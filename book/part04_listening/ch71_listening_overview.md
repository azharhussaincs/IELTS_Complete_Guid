## Theory / Explanation

### 71.1 What IELTS Listening Measures

IELTS Listening assesses your ability to understand **spoken English in everyday and academic contexts**. You are not tested on background knowledge of a topic; you are tested on whether you can:

1. **Follow main ideas** in conversations and monologues.
2. **Catch specific factual detail** (names, numbers, dates, places).
3. **Recognise attitudes, opinions, and purpose** (especially Sections 3–4).
4. **Track discourse organisation** — how a speaker moves from point to point.
5. **Handle paraphrase** — the answer rarely matches the audio word-for-word.

The same Listening test is used for **Academic** and **General Training** candidates. Your Listening score contributes equally to both versions.

### 71.2 Test Structure at a Glance

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Sections | 4 |
| Questions | 40 |
| Approx. duration | 30 minutes (+ 10 minutes transfer on paper; see below) |
| Recording | Each section heard **once only** |
| Voices | Mix of monologues and conversations; various English accents |
| Question types | Form/note/table completion, multiple choice, matching, map/plan/diagram, sentence/summary completion, short answer |
| Scoring | 1 mark per correct answer; no penalty for wrong guesses |

**Section difficulty curve:**

| Section | Context | Typical speakers | Difficulty |
|---------|---------|------------------|------------|
| 1 | Social/everyday | Two people | Lower |
| 2 | Social/monologue | One person | Medium |
| 3 | Educational/training | Two to four people | Medium–high |
| 4 | Academic lecture | One person | Highest |

**Why the curve matters:** Many candidates treat Section 1 as "easy" and over-focus on Section 4. Band 7+ requires **near-perfect Section 1–2** plus strong Section 3–4 — you cannot recover five lost marks in Section 1 through Section 4 heroics.

### 71.3 Paper vs Computer Delivery

**Paper-based test (PBT):**
- You write answers on the question booklet during listening.
- After the recording ends, you receive **10 minutes** to transfer answers to the answer sheet.
- Spelling and grammar on the answer sheet must be correct.

**Computer-delivered test (CDT):**
- You type answers during listening; there is **no separate 10-minute transfer** at the end of Listening (transfer time may appear between modules in the full exam).
- Typing speed and accuracy matter; practice typing names and numbers quickly.

**Strategic implication:** PBT candidates must rehearse **legible handwriting under time pressure**. CDT candidates must rehearse **keyboard entry without mis-clicks**.

### 71.4 How Raw Scores Convert to Bands

Raw scores (out of 40) map to band scores via official conversion tables that **vary slightly by test form**. Approximate guides:

| Raw /40 | Typical band |
|---------|--------------|
| 39–40 | 9.0 |
| 37–38 | 8.5 |
| 35–36 | 8.0 |
| 32–34 | 7.5 |
| 30–31 | 7.0 |
| 26–29 | 6.5 |
| 23–25 | 6.0 |

**Why "approximately":** A form with harder Section 4 may allow 33/40 for Band 7.5. Treat mock conversions as **trends**, not guarantees.

### 71.5 The Listening Timeline (Minute by Minute)

Understanding the invisible clock reduces panic.

**Before Section 1:**
- Instructions explain the test.
- Example question played — use this to confirm sound level and question layout.

**Between sections:**
- Short pause to read upcoming questions (typically 30–60 seconds depending on item count).
- **This is active study time**, not rest.

**During each section:**
- Audio plays continuously; you cannot pause or rewind.
- Questions generally follow **audio order** (with rare exceptions in matching).

**After Section 4:**
- PBT: 10-minute transfer.
- CDT: check typed entries immediately.

**Why timeline awareness helps:** Candidates who "wait for the conversation to start" waste the most valuable seconds — the preview window.

### 71.6 Core Micro-Skills

#### Prediction

Before audio begins, read questions and guess:
- **Word class** (noun, number, adjective)
- **Topic field** (price, date, location, reason)
- **Synonyms** the speaker might use

**Example:** Question says *"cost of membership"*. Audio may say *"annual fee"* or *"subscription charge"*.

#### Signpost tracking

Speakers signal structure:

- *"First of all…" / "Moving on to…" / "The main problem was…"*
- Contrast: *"However…" / "On the other hand…"*
- Correction: *"Actually, I mean…" / "Sorry, not Tuesday — Wednesday."*

**Why corrections trap candidates:** The first number mentioned is often a **distractor**; the corrected value is the answer.

#### Note-taking vs answer-writing

In fast sections, jot **minimal cues** (e.g., `Wed`, `£45`, `lib ref desk`) then expand during transfer. Avoid writing full sentences during Section 4 unless you can touch-type quickly.

#### Spelling discipline

Misspelled correct answers score **zero**. British and American spelling are usually both accepted if listed on the official key — when unsure, use the spelling in the question paper if provided.

### 71.8 Accent Exposure Strategy

IELTS uses **British, Australian, North American, and New Zealand** English among others. You are not marked on accent production — only comprehension.

**Training plan:**
- Week 1–2: identify your weakest accent (often Australian or regional British).
- Week 3–4: deliberate exposure — podcasts, news clips, lecture excerpts.
- Week 5+: mixed-accent mock sections under timed conditions.

**Why accent training is not optional at Band 8+:** Section 1 may feature Australian English while Section 4 uses British academic intonation; fatigue increases mishearing risk.

### 71.9 Common Failure Patterns

| Pattern | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Spelling loss | Knows word, loses mark | Personal spelling list + dictation |
| Distractor capture | Writes first heard option | Wait for correction / full clause |
| Preview neglect | Enters section cold | Strict 30-second question skim rule |
| Section 4 freeze | Misses Q31–35, recovers late | Skip-flag-return protocol |
| Transfer error | Right booklet, wrong sheet | Double-check question numbers |
| Over-writing | Exceeds word limit | Count words before writing |

### 71.10 Integration with Other Modules

Listening feeds:
- **Speaking** — rhythm, chunking, discourse markers.
- **Writing** — spelling of academic vocabulary (Chapter 69 AWL).
- **Reading** — paraphrase recognition transfers directly.

**Weekly integration drill:** After each Listening mock, extract 10 new collocations into your vocabulary notebook (Chapter 44).

### 71.11 Equipment and Test-Day Listening

- Confirm **headphones** fit and volume during the example.
- Raise hand immediately for audio issues — you cannot replay.
- Ignore other candidates' pacing; trust your question numbers.
- PBT: bring **sharp pencil**; eraser clean.
- CDT: practice on similar keyboard if possible.

### 71.12 Building a Listening Study Cycle

**Phase A — Accuracy (untimed):**
- Single section replay (non-official practice only) to analyse paraphrase.
- Transcript shadowing for weak accents.

**Phase B — Speed (timed):**
- Full test weekly; no pauses.
- Section 4 isolation drills twice weekly.

**Phase C — Exam simulation:**
- Full LRW morning mock including transfer.
- Post-mortem: error log by type (Chapter 6).

- Customer name: ___________
- Membership type: ___________

**Prediction notes (written in margin):**
- Name = proper noun, spelling letter-by-letter likely
- Membership = gold/silver/basic OR annual/monthly

### Example 2: Signpost in Section 3

Audio: *"We initially planned the survey for March, but recruitment delays pushed it back to April."*

Question: *Survey month: ___________

**Trap:** March (first mention). **Answer:** April (corrected timeline).

### Example 3: Word Limit

Question: *NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER*

Audio: *"The workshop is in the main lecture hall."*

**Wrong:** `main lecture hall` (3 words). **Right:** `lecture hall` OR `main hall` — choose the pair that preserves meaning and fits limit.

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Raw Score to Band Goal

**Target:** Band 7.0 (typically 30–31/40).

**Mock result:** S1 9/10, S2 8/10, S3 7/10, S4 5/10 → 29/40 = Band 6.5.

**Diagnosis:** Section 4 pace + note density, not overall "listening ability."

**Plan:** Two Section 4-only tests per week; AWL vocabulary for lecture topics (science, urban planning); maintain S1–2 perfection drills.

### Worked Example 2: Transfer Error Prevention (PBT)

Candidate writes answers in booklet Q11–20 during Section 2. During transfer, shifts all answers **one line down** from Q12 onward.

**Prevention rule:** Transfer **section by section**, verbally confirming *"Section 2, question 11"* before writing each block.

### Worked Example 3: Skip-Flag-Return

In Section 4, candidate misses Q33 (complex paraphrase). Instead of rewinding mentally (impossible), they:
1. Flag Q33 in booklet.
2. Capture Q34–36 immediately.
3. If Q37 paraphrase echoes Q33 vocabulary, attempt fill-in.

**Why it works:** Audio order means **later context sometimes clarifies earlier gaps** — but only if you stay forward-moving.

## Review

- Listening = 4 sections, 40 questions, **one play only**, ~30 minutes.
- Difficulty rises; **Sections 1–2 are scoring foundations**.
- Master **preview, paraphrase, spelling, word limits, distractor awareness**.
- PBT transfer time needs **procedure rehearsal**.
- Accent variety is deliberate — train mixed exposure.
- Error log by **type**, not "I am bad at Listening."

## Exam Tips

> **Exam Strategy:** Use the **example question** at the start to set volume — not after Section 1 begins.

> **Exam Strategy:** Never leave a blank: **no negative marking** — intelligent guesses after elimination beat empty boxes.

> **Exam Strategy:** If you miss an answer, **move on immediately** — staying stuck costs the next two questions.

> **Exam Strategy:** Section 1 deserves **100% focus** — these marks are the most accessible in the entire test.

## Band 9 Tips

> **Band 9 Secret:** Band 9 listeners treat **question preview as a micro-reading test** — they underline limit words (ONLY, NOT, NO MORE THAN) and predict grammar before the first syllable plays.

> **Band 9 Secret:** High-band scorers maintain a **section score ledger** across mocks; they refuse to proceed until Sections 1–2 average 19/20 or higher.

> **Band 9 Secret:** Elite performers **verbalise corrections** in practice ("March—no, April") until the habit becomes automatic under exam noise.
