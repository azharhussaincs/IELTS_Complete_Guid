## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Recognise form, note, and table completion formats across all four Listening sections.
- Apply word-limit rules precisely, including articles, numbers, and hyphenated compounds.
- Capture names, addresses, phone numbers, dates, prices, and spellings with exam-ready accuracy.
- Use row/column navigation in tables to stay synchronised with audio order.
- Predict answer grammar from gap position and surrounding syntax.
- Avoid distractors in numeric sequences, corrected details, and listed options.
- Complete high-frequency Section 1–2 forms at near-perfect accuracy consistently.

## Theory / Explanation

### 72.1 What Are Completion Tasks?

Form, note, and table completion items present a **partially filled document**. You write missing words or numbers heard in the recording. The document may resemble:

- **Forms:** membership applications, booking sheets, registration cards.
- **Notes:** lecture notes, meeting minutes, interview summaries.
- **Tables:** schedules, comparison charts, survey results.

Despite different layouts, the **cognitive task is identical:** map spoken information to a labelled gap while respecting constraints.

### 72.2 Why Completion Dominates Section 1

Section 1 almost always opens with a **social dialogue** completing a form. Examiners test:

| Skill | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Spelling | Surnames, street names |
| Numbers | Prices, phone, postcodes |
| Dates/times | Appointments, opening hours |
| Classification | Membership type, room category |
| Listening for correction | "No, the 14th, not the 4th" |

**Band impact:** Ten Section 1 questions reward **precision**, not inference. Losing four marks here often drops an entire band half-step.

### 72.3 Word-Limit Rules (Non-Negotiable)

Instructions appear in CAPS above each question group:

| Instruction | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| ONE WORD ONLY | Single word; numbers as digits usually OK |
| NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS | 1–2 words |
| NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER | Words + numbers combined; count carefully |
| NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER | Very common in Sections 1–2 |

**Rules:**
- **Hyphenated compounds** may count as one word (*well-known*, *mid-range*).
- **Numbers** expressed as digits (45) or words (forty-five) unless specified.
- **Articles** (*a*, *the*) count as words unless question already includes them.
- Exceeding the limit → **zero mark**, even if content correct.

### 72.4 Grammar Prediction from Gap Position

| Gap context | Expected answer |
|-------------|-----------------|
| `The ___ is broken.` | Noun |
| `It was extremely ___.` | Adjective/adverb |
| `They decided to ___.` | Verb (base/infinitive) |
| `Meet at ___ on Monday.` | Place/time |
| `Cost: £___` | Number |
| `Contact: ___@email.com` | Email fragment |

**Why grammar helps:** If you hear an adjective but gap needs noun, you may have misaligned question numbers — immediate red flag.

### 72.5 Forms: Field-by-Field Strategy

**Step 1 — Scan field labels** (30 seconds): underline keywords (*surname*, *deposit*, *preferred*).

**Step 2 — Predict content type** per field.

**Step 3 — During audio:**
- Write in **pencil** lightly; confirm spelling when speaker repeats.
- For letters, use **block capitals** on answer sheet if unsure of cursive clarity.

**Step 4 — Corrections:** When speaker self-corrects, **cross out gently**, write final value.

### 72.6 Tables: Row and Column Tracking

Tables add **visual complexity**. Audio still follows question order **left-to-right, top-to-bottom** (usually).

**Technique:**
1. Identify **headers** (days, prices, features).
2. Place finger on **first gap cell** before audio.
3. Move cell-by-cell; never jump ahead visually.

**Trap:** Speaker discusses Row C while you stare at Row B — misalignment cascade.

### 72.7 Notes: Abbreviation During Listening

Notes completion (Sections 2–4) allows **shorthand in booklet** during listening, but answer sheet needs **full acceptable form** within word limit.

| Shorthand | Transfer as |
|-----------|-------------|
| gov | government (if one word limit fails — use longer only if allowed) |
| approx | approximately (check limit!) |
| temp | temperature |

**Caution:** Transfer exactly what fits the limit; trim intelligently.

### 72.8 Numbers, Dates, and Money

#### Numbers
- **teen vs ty:** 13 vs 30 — stress pattern differs; practice minimal pairs.
- **Double/triple:** "double six" = 66.
- **Thousands:** 1,500 may be "fifteen hundred" or "one thousand five hundred."

#### Dates
British order often **day-month-year** in speech: *the third of November*.
Watch **ordinal vs cardinal**: *May 15th* not *May 50*.

#### Money
Currency may precede or follow: *£45* / *45 pounds*.
**Decoys:** deposit vs total vs monthly fee — label match essential.

### 72.9 Spelling Patterns in IELTS

High-frequency spelling traps:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Double letters | accommodation, committee, beginning |
| -ance/-ence | attendance, licence (noun UK) |
| Proper nouns | Kettering, Woolwich, Fitzgerald |
| Job titles | receptionist, coordinator |

**System:** Maintain personal **"Listening Spelling Hit List"** from every mock.

### 72.10 Distractors Specific to Completion

1. **Option lists:** Speaker mentions A, B, C; answer is B — others are traps.
2. **Partial match:** Question asks for *reason*; speaker gives reason embedded in long clause — extract core noun phrase within limit.
3. **Homophones:** *site* vs *sight*, *stationary* vs *stationery*.
4. **Corrected info:** First value wrong; second right.

### 72.11 Section Distribution

| Section | Completion style |
|---------|------------------|
| 1 | Full form common |
| 2 | Notes or table (monologue) |
| 3 | Notes in discussion context |
| 4 | Lecture notes or summary gaps |

Difficulty rises through **vocabulary abstraction**, not necessarily different instructions.

### 72.12 Transfer Discipline (PBT)

- Complete **one section block** before moving to next on answer sheet.
- Say question number aloud silently: "Eighteen…"
- Check **plural endings** heard at word end (*requirements* not *requirement*).

## Examples

### Example 1: Form Gap with Article Trap

**Question:** Equipment needed: ___________ (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS)

**Audio:** *"You'll need a helmet and gloves, but we provide the harness."*

**Wrong:** `a helmet` (two words but question may expect equipment not provided — read full dialogue).

If question asks what **student must bring** → **helmet** OR **helmet gloves** depending on full context.

### Example 2: Table Completion

| Day | Instructor | Level |
|-----|------------|-------|
| Mon | Sarah | beginner |
| Wed | ___ | intermediate |
| Sat | Marco | ___ |

**Audio:** *"Wednesday classes are taken over by James this term… Saturday remains advanced with Marco."*

Answers: **James**, **advanced**.

### Example 3: Note Completion with Paraphrase

**Note:** Main cause of delay: ___________

**Audio:** *"The project stalled chiefly because funding approval arrived late."*

Answer: **funding approval** (two words) or **late funding** — choose fit within limit matching question wording style.

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Phone Number

**Audio:** *"My number is oh one two three, four double eight, nine oh one."*

Breakdown:
- oh = 0
- double eight = 88
- nine oh one = 901

**Answer:** 0123 488 901 (format as heard; spaces optional unless specified).

**WHY wrong answers fail:** 0123 489 801 swaps digits; missing "double" loses a digit.

### Worked Example 2: Corrected Date

**Audio:** *"Let's book the fifteenth of June… actually, make that the eighteenth — the fifteenth is fully booked."*

**Question:** Booking date: ___________

**Answer:** 18 June / June 18 / 18th June (accept test conventions).

**WHY:** Fifteenth is explicit distractor before correction.

### Worked Example 3: Word Limit Trim

**Audio:** *"The library's extended study zone is on the second floor."*

**Limit:** ONE WORD ONLY

**Wrong:** second floor (two words)

**Right:** **second** if question is *Floor: ___* OR re-read question — if gap is *Location: ___* with two-word limit → **second floor**.

**Lesson:** Gap label determines extraction.

## Review

- Form/note/table = same skill: **gap filling with limits**.
- **Preview labels**, predict grammar, track order.
- **Spelling and numbers** decide Section 1 bands.
- Tables need **cell-by-cell** tracking.
- **Corrections** override first mention.
- Transfer section-by-section on PBT.

## Exam Tips

> **Exam Strategy:** When the speaker **spells** a word, write each letter immediately — do not wait for the end.

> **Exam Strategy:** For prices, write the **currency label** in the question if already printed; don't duplicate unless gap requires it.

> **Exam Strategy:** If two answers seem possible, choose the one **last confirmed** by the speaker.

## Band 9 Tips

> **Band 9 Secret:** Band 9 candidates **pre-write probable field formats** in margins (e.g., `___ Rd`, `BN_ ___`) so handwriting stays compact during fast dictation.

> **Band 9 Secret:** They **never transfer ambiguous digits** — if "15 or 50," they flag and resolve from context before leaving the section.

> **Band 9 Secret:** Section 1 completion is treated as a **spelling exam with background noise** — zero tolerance mindset.
