## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Answer direct short-answer questions that require brief factual responses from the recording.
- Apply word limits (typically NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER) with precision.
- Distinguish short-answer from sentence completion when questions are full interrogatives.
- Locate answers efficiently using question word cues (who, what, when, where, why, how).
- Handle multi-part questions where one audio segment supplies several answers in sequence.
- Avoid copying unnecessary surrounding words that exceed limits.
- Integrate short-answer skills with note-taking for fast Section 2–4 retrieval.

## Theory / Explanation

### 77.1 What Are Short-Answer Questions?

Short-answer (SAQ) items ask **direct questions**:

- *What time does the shop close?*
- *Who conducted the research?*
- *How many species were recorded?*

You write a **brief answer** — not a full sentence — respecting stated word limits.

**Frequency:** Less common than completion or MCQ but appear in **Sections 2–4**, sometimes grouped 3–5 questions.

### 77.2 Question Word Routing

| Question word | Answer type | Listen for |
|---------------|-------------|------------|
| Who | Person/organisation | Proper nouns, roles |
| What | Thing/concept | Nouns/noun phrases |
| When | Time/date | Numbers, months, periods |
| Where | Place | Locations, directions |
| Why | Reason | *because*, *due to*, *so that* |
| How many/much | Quantity | Numbers |
| How | Method/manner | Process verbs, *by + -ing* |

**Preview:** Circle question word; predict answer **category** before audio.

### 77.3 Word Limits in SAQ

Typical: **NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER**

| Question | Audio | Acceptable |
|----------|-------|------------|
| What material? | "Made of recycled glass" | recycled glass (2) |
| How old? | "About twelve centuries" | 12 centuries / 1200 years — check context |
| Who? | "Dr Patel from Leeds" | Dr Patel / Patel — if THREE WORDS max |

**Articles:** *the*, *a* count unless question structure includes them separately.

### 77.4 SAQ vs Sentence Completion

| Feature | SAQ | Sentence completion |
|---------|-----|---------------------|
| Form | Question sentence | Statement with gap |
| Grammar check | Answer standalone | Must fit sentence frame |
| Preview focus | Question word | Syntax around gap |

Same listening skill; different **verification method**.

### 77.5 Answer Extraction Rules

1. **Minimal sufficient answer** — don't add padding.
2. **Exact spelling** for proper nouns.
3. **Number format** — digits usually fine.
4. **Hyphenated** compounds = one word.

**Wrong:** Question asks *Where* → answering with full clause *"in the library on campus"* when *library* suffices and limit tight.

### 77.6 Section 2 Short Answers

Monologue contexts: museum info, tour, facility rules.

Answers often **straightforward detail** — closing time, price, eligibility.

**Trap:** Speaker mentions multiple times; question asks **specific** event (evening lecture vs general hours).

### 77.7 Section 3–4 Short Answers

Higher paraphrase:

**Q:** *What problem did the initial survey reveal?*  
**Audio:** *"Respondents struggled to understand the terminology."*  
**A:** **terminology** / **confusing terminology** (within limit)

### 77.8 Sequential Answer Clusters

One audio stretch may answer Q18, Q19, Q20 in order — stay aligned.

If Q19 missed, **do not delay** — Q20 may still be clear; return if time at section end (mental note only; no replay).

### 77.9 Why Questions

Reason answers often follow:

- *because of + noun*
- *due to + noun*
- *so they could + verb*

Extract **core noun phrase** within limit: *because of funding delays* → **funding delays** (two words).

### 77.10 Spelling and Numbers Again

SAQ loses marks identically to completion on spelling.

**Phone/postcode** items rare in SAQ but **dates** common.

### 77.11 Guessing in SAQ

Semantic guess from topic if missed:

- Biology lecture + *What gas* → maybe **carbon dioxide** if carbon context mentioned elsewhere — risky; better skip-flag.

### 77.12 Practice Materials Design

When creating SAQ drills, ensure **one clear answer** per question — ambiguous keys harm learning.

## Examples

### Example 1: Who

**Q:** Who manages the volunteer programme?  
**Audio:** *"Sarah Chen coordinates all volunteer shifts."*  
**A:** **Sarah Chen** (two words — within three)

### Example 2: How many

**Q:** How many workshops are offered?  
**Audio:** *"We run four workshops each term."*  
**A:** **4** / **four**

### Example 3: Why

**Q:** Why was the event rescheduled?  
**Audio:** *"Heavy snow made the road unsafe."*  
**A:** **heavy snow** / **unsafe roads** — choose best noun phrase within limit

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Minimal Answer

**Q:** Where should visitors park? (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS)  
**Audio:** *"Use the north car park behind the sports hall."*

**Too long:** north car park behind sports hall  
**Correct:** **north car park** OR **car park** — if TWO WORDS max, **north car park** = 3 words → **car park** only if question accepts generic; **north car park** is 3 — trim to **north carpark** invalid.

**Correct TWO WORDS:** **north car** wrong semantics → **sports hall** wrong (behind hall not at hall) → **north park** odd → Best: **north car park** exceeds → re-read limit.

If **TWO WORDS:** **north carpark** (hyphen?) → **north lot** if paraphrase allowed — usually **car park** (2 words) if sufficient.

**Lesson:** Limit drives extraction ruthlessly.

### Worked Example 2: Paraphrase

**Q:** What skill do interns develop?  
**Audio:** *"They gain confidence presenting to clients."*  
**A:** **presenting** / **client presenting** — match limit

### Worked Example 3: Distractor Times

**Q:** When does the film start?  
**Audio:** *"Doors open at six thirty; screening begins at seven fifteen."*  
**A:** **7:15** / **seven fifteen** — not 6:30

## Appendix 77.A — WH- Question Transformation

Convert statements to SAQ (practice paraphrase):

| Statement | SAQ |
|-----------|-----|
| The lab closes at six pm. | When does the lab close? |
| Dr Nguyen led the trial. | Who led the trial? |
| Funding came from a charity. | Where did funding come from? / Who funded? |
| They chose method B because of cost. | Why was method B chosen? |

Write **10 transformations** weekly from Chapter 79 transcripts — builds reverse paraphrase skill for Section 3–4.

## Extended Theory — Multi-Answer Scope

When question asks for **one cause** but audio lists three, extract **first emphasised** or **explicitly labelled main**:

> *"Three factors mattered — **cost**, distance, and timing — but **cost** dominated decisions."*

**Answer:** **cost** — **WHY not distance/timing:** dominance marker "dominated" signals primary.

## Appendix 77.B — Minimal Answer Decision Tree

```
Question word identified?
 ├─ Who → proper noun / role noun
 ├─ When → time/date (shortest valid)
 ├─ Where → place name (not full address unless needed)
 ├─ How many → number (+ unit if essential)
 ├─ Why → cause noun phrase core
 └─ What → thing/concept noun
Check word limit → trim to minimal sufficient → verify spelling
```

Print and use during first five practice tests until **internalised**.

---

## Appendix 77.C — SAQ in Full Test Context

In 40-question tests, SAQ items typically cluster in **Section 2–3** (6–8 total). Missing one SAQ rarely changes band alone; missing six SAQ because of **systematic limit violations** drops band — treat word limits as strictly as spelling.

**Band 9 rule:** After writing SAQ, **count words on fingers** before moving to next question — 2 seconds per item, zero limit errors over full prep cycle.

**Final note:** Short-answer items reward concise precision — practise trimming adjectives until only the essential noun or number remains. This habit alone can recover two to three marks per test lost to word-limit violations.
