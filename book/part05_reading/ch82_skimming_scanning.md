## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Define skimming, scanning, and intensive reading and deploy each at the correct stage of the IELTS Reading test.
- Extract keywords and search terms from questions using systematic annotation rules.
- Recognise synonym chains and reference chains that connect questions to distant passage locations.
- Avoid the "keyword trap" where identical words appear in distractors but not in answers.
- Combine skimming for structure with scanning for detail within a single passage under time pressure.
- Apply keyword transformation rules for names, numbers, dates, and abstract concepts.
- Evaluate your own reading speed and adjust strategies rather than attempting to read every word.

## Theory / Explanation

### 82.1 Three Reading Speeds

IELTS Reading demands **three distinct reading speeds**, not one:

| Mode | Purpose | What the eyes do | Typical duration |
|------|---------|------------------|------------------|
| **Skimming** | Gist, structure, tone | Move quickly; read topic sentences, titles, transitions | 1–2 min per passage |
| **Scanning** | Locate specific information | Hunt for keywords and paraphrases; ignore ir relevant paragraphs | Seconds per item |
| **Intensive reading** | Understand logic of a difficult clause | Slow, parse grammar, resolve reference | 15–45 sec per segment |

**Why three speeds:** Candidates who skim when they should scan miss detail; candidates who scan when they should skim fail headings and main-idea items.

### 82.2 Skimming: The Architecture of a Passage

Skimming answers: **What is this passage about, and how is it organised?**

**Skimming protocol:**

1. Read the **title** and any subtitle or epigraph.
2. Read the **first sentence** of each paragraph (often the topic sentence in academic prose).
3. Read the **last sentence** of the introduction and conclusion paragraphs.
4. Note **signpost words**: `firstly`, `in contrast`, `the implications`, `in conclusion`.
5. Ignore examples, statistics, and names on first pass unless the question targets them.

**Paragraph function labels** (write in margin):

- INTRO — thesis or problem statement
- HIST — background
- ARG — claim
- EVID — study/data/example
- CONC — counterargument or limitation
- SUM — conclusion or future direction

**Why label paragraphs:** Matching headings and matching information become faster when each paragraph's **function** is pre-identified.

### 82.3 Scanning: Locating the Needle

Scanning answers: **Where in the passage is this specific information?**

**Scanning protocol:**

1. **Annotate the question** — underline 2–4 keywords (nouns, names, numbers, distinctive terms).
2. **Predict paraphrase** — write a synonym beside each keyword if possible.
3. **Visual search** — eyes move in Z-pattern or paragraph-by-paragraph until anchor found.
4. **Read intensively** — 2–3 sentences around the match to confirm answer.

**Scan anchors:**

- **Proper nouns** — capitalised names, places, organisations (often unchanged in text and question).
- **Numbers** — dates, percentages, quantities (may appear as words: "half" = 50%).
- **Distinctive terminology** — technical terms less likely to be paraphrased.
- **Rare words** — low-frequency nouns that stand out visually.

**Why anchors work:** Even in 900-word passages, anchors reduce search space from 100% to ~10% of text.

### 82.4 Keyword Selection Rules

From any question, extract keywords using this hierarchy:

**Priority 1 — Unparaphrasable:** names, numbers, place names, titles  
**Priority 2 — Content words:** nouns and main verbs carrying meaning  
**Priority 3 — Modifiers:** only if they distinguish similar concepts (e.g., "annual" vs "monthly")

**Delete as keywords:**

- Grammar words: the, a, is, of, have  
- Generic verbs: make, do, get, be (unless central to meaning)  
- Words repeated throughout passage (e.g., "technology" in a tech passage)

**Example:**

**Question:** "What did Henderson discover about nocturnal feeding in 2019?"

**Keywords:** Henderson | nocturnal feeding | 2019  
**Paraphrase predictions:** Henderson (unchanged) | night-time eating | 2019 (unchanged)

### 82.5 The Keyword Trap

Examiners deliberately place **question words** in wrong sections to trap scanners who match words without meaning.

**Trap type 1 — Same word, wrong context**

Passage A discusses "plastic **recycling** rates." Passage B discusses "recycling **water** in agriculture." Question about water recycling — word "recycling" appears in both; only B is correct.

**Trap type 2 — Distractor in multiple choice**

Option B repeats three words from the passage but reverses causal direction.

**Trap type 3 — False comfort in TFNG**

Statement includes "always" and passage says "often" — same topic, different quantifier → FALSE, not TRUE because keywords match.

**Defence:** After locating keywords, read the **full sentence** and compare **propositional content**, not word overlap.

### 82.6 Synonym Chains and Reference Tracking

Advanced scanning requires following **reference chains**:

**Passage excerpt:** "The **expedition** reached the plateau in March. **It** documented seventeen species previously unknown to science. **These findings** were later disputed."

- "It" = expedition  
- "These findings" = seventeen species documentation  

**Question:** "Which journey recorded new species?" → expedition (via chain, not keyword in question)

**Synonym chain example:**

| Passage term | Question term |
|--------------|---------------|
| remuneration | pay |
| workforce | employees |
| terminate | end |

Build active synonym networks from Part 3 vocabulary chapters.

### 82.7 Skim-Scan Integration per Question Type

| Question type | Skim first? | Scan method |
|---------------|-------------|-------------|
| Matching headings | Yes — full passage skim | Re-read paragraph openings |
| TFNG | Light skim for structure | Scan for statement subject |
| Matching information | Yes — label paragraphs A–G | Scan question keywords per paragraph |
| MCQ | Optional | Scan stem keywords; read surrounding context |
| Summary gaps | Skim summary structure | Scan for grammar + meaning fit |
| Short answer | No full skim needed | Direct scan for question words |

### 82.8 Eye Movement and Physical Technique

- **Finger or pencil guide** — acceptable; keeps line focus under stress.
- **Do not mouth words** — subvocalisation slows speed; practice silent reading.
- **Peripheral vision batching** — read phrases, not single words.
- **Return sweep** — after scanning paragraph, note if topic shifted before next paragraph.

**Why physical technique matters:** Band gains from eliminating regressions (re-reading same line unnecessarily).

### 82.9 When to Abandon Scanning

If anchor not found in 90 seconds:

1. Mark question flagged.
2. Move to next question (often later text provides context).
3. Return in Pass 3 with broader skim knowledge.

**Why:** Sequential obsession on one item costs 3–4 easier marks elsewhere.

## Examples

### Example 1: Keyword Extraction

**Question:** "According to the passage, why did the Meridan project fail to attract investors?"

**Keywords:** Meridan project | fail | attract investors  
**Scan for:** Meridan (name), investment/funding/finance vocabulary, failure verbs (collapsed, withdrew, stalled)

### Example 2: Skimming Labels

**Passage on renewable energy:**

- ¶1 INTRO — climate targets  
- ¶2 HIST — fossil fuel dominance  
- ¶3 ARG — solar cost reduction  
- ¶4 EVID — 2022 study data  
- ¶5 CONC — grid storage problem  
- ¶6 SUM — policy recommendations  

Headings question: ¶5 likely matches "The obstacle to continuous supply" not "Solar panel manufacturing."

### Example 3: Keyword Trap

**Statement:** Training programmes always improve retention.  
**Passage:** "Structured onboarding **often** correlates with higher retention."

Same topic (training/onboarding, retention) — but **always** vs **often** → **FALSE**.

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Full Scan Sequence

**Question 14:** "What material did Kowalski use in the 2017 prototype?"

**Step 1 — Keywords:** Kowalski | 2017 | prototype | material  
**Step 2 — Scan:** Find "Kowalski" → paragraph 4, line 3  
**Step 3 — Intensive read:** "...Kowalski's 2017 prototype employed **a graphene composite** rather than conventional polymers."  
**Step 4 — Answer:** graphene composite (check word limit: TWO WORDS → "graphene composite")

### Worked Example 2: Skim Then Headings

**Paragraph opening:** "Despite initial enthusiasm, several municipalities abandoned bike-share schemes within two years."

**Heading options:**  
A. Financial incentives for cyclists  
B. Early withdrawal from rental programmes  
C. Health benefits of urban cycling  

**Process:** Skim signals "abandoned" + "within two years" = early withdrawal → **B**. Option C is topic-related trap (cycling mentioned elsewhere).

### Worked Example 3: Reference Chain

**Passage:** "The **committee** issued revised guidelines. **They** mandated quarterly audits. **This requirement** provoked opposition from small firms."

**Question:** "Who mandated quarterly audits?"  
**Chain:** they = committee → Answer: the committee

## Review

- **Skim** for structure; **scan** for detail; **read intensively** only at answer location.
- Select keywords by hierarchy: names/numbers first, then content words.
- **Keyword trap:** word match ≠ correct answer — verify proposition.
- Track **reference chains** (it, this, these, such).
- Label paragraph **function** during skim for headings and matching information.
- Use **90-second rule** — flag and move to protect overall score.

## Exam Tips

1. **Underline question keywords** in the first 5 seconds of each item.
2. **Circle names and dates** in passage margin when they appear during skim.
3. **Never scan for "development"** alone — combine with second anchor.
4. **Headings skim:** first + last sentence of each paragraph only.
5. **Numbers appear as words** — scan for both "50%" and "half."
6. **If two paragraphs match**, compare both intensively — one will fail a detail test.
7. **Pencil guide** is allowed and professional — use it.
8. **Do not skim examples** for headings — they rarely define paragraph purpose.
9. **Question order** often follows passage order — use sequential scanning when stuck.
10. **Build AWL synonym networks** (Chapter 69) — scanning speed depends on them.

## Challenge

**The Blind Scan Drill:** Take 10 detail questions from a passage you have **not** skimmed. Scan only — no prior skim — and record accuracy. Then skim 90 seconds and repeat same questions. Compare times and scores. Goal: skim adds accuracy without adding more than 2 minutes total per passage.

**Speed target:** Locate anchor for any named entity within 30 seconds in Passage 1–2 texts.

## Band 9 Strategies

1. **Simultaneous structure map:** While scanning for Q7, update mental paragraph map if Q7 reveals new structural information — no separate re-read needed.

2. **Pre-emptive paraphrase:** Before eyes leave the question, generate 2–3 synonym candidates; scan for all simultaneously.

3. **Negation spotting during scan:** Band 9 readers notice `not`, `un-`, `fail to`, `lack` in answer zone instantly — critical for TFNG and MCQ elimination.

4. **Discourse-level skim:** Identify whether passage is **descriptive**, **argumentative**, or **problem-solution** in first 20 seconds — predicts where opinions vs facts cluster.

5. **Minimal intensive zone:** Read minimum window (often one sentence before and after match) — extra reading introduces distractor material.

6. **Cross-paragraph scan:** Matching information may require scanning entire passage once with question list visible — batch scan all questions per passage.

7. **Reject regression habit:** Band 9 candidates re-read only flagged items in Pass 3, never compulsively.

8. **Keyword deletion mastery:** Strip questions to skeleton; prevents fixation on repeated low-value terms.

9. **Visual chunking:** Read in sense groups (`The 2017 prototype / employed a graphene composite / rather than conventional polymers`) — faster and more accurate.

10. **Integrate Listening skill:** Chapter 78 paraphrase tracking in Listening trains Reading scan recognition — cross-train both modules.
