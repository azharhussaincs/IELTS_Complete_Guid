## Theory / Explanation

### 83.1 Two Related but Distinct Question Types

**Identifying Information (TFNG)** — Does the **statement** agree with the **facts in the passage**?

| Answer | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **TRUE** | Statement matches passage information (may be paraphrased) |
| **FALSE** | Statement contradicts passage information |
| **NOT GIVEN** | Passage neither confirms nor contradicts the statement |

**Identifying Writer's Views/Claims (YNNG)** — Does the **statement** agree with the **writer's views or claims**?

| Answer | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **YES** | Writer holds this view or makes this claim |
| **NO** | Writer expresses the opposite view or contradicts the claim |
| **NOT GIVEN** | Writer's position on this specific claim is not stated |

**Critical distinction:** TFNG can be answered from **any information in the text**, including reported facts from studies. YNNG requires evidence of **authorial stance** — attitude, evaluation, recommendation, or explicit claim attributed to the writer.

**Example:**

Passage: "Critics argue that the policy is ineffective."  
Statement: "The policy is ineffective." → TFNG: **NOT GIVEN** (critics' view, not confirmed as fact). YNNG depends on whether the **writer** endorses critics — if writer adds "This criticism is well founded," then YES for writer's view.

### 83.2 The Verification Protocol

Apply this sequence for **every** statement:

**Step 1 — Decompose the statement**  
Identify subject, verb, object, qualifiers (all, some, never, may, most).

**Step 2 — Locate the topic**  
Scan for subject or paraphrase (Chapter 82).

**Step 3 — Compare propositions**  
Ask: "If I accept the passage as true, is the statement necessarily true, necessarily false, or undetermined?"

**Step 4 — Check qualifiers separately**  
Even if topic matches, `always` vs `sometimes` → FALSE.

**Step 5 — NG test**  
Ask: "Does the passage say anything about **this specific aspect**?" If one element is missing → NG.

**Why protocol beats intuition:** Under time pressure, intuition overuses NG (safe guess) or TRUE (keyword match).

### 83.3 TRUE/YES Conditions

Statement is TRUE/YES when:

- Passage states the same proposition in paraphrase.
- Passage states a specific case that satisfies a general statement in the question (careful — sometimes this is FALSE if question overgeneralises).
- Logical entailment: passage says "all participants completed the survey"; statement "every participant finished the survey" → TRUE.

**Not TRUE merely because:**

- Topic is discussed.
- Some words match.
- You know from outside knowledge.

**Outside knowledge rule:** IELTS ignores your encyclopaedic knowledge. If passage doesn't say it, it's NG even if "everyone knows" it.

### 83.4 FALSE/NO Conditions

Statement is FALSE/NO when:

- Passage states the **opposite** (antonym, reversed causality, different agent).
- Passage provides a **counterexample** to an absolute in the statement.
- Passage says X happened; statement says X did not happen.
- Quantifier mismatch where passage clearly restricts scope: "some" in text vs "all" in statement → FALSE (not NG) when passage explicitly limits scope.

**Common FALSE patterns:**

| Statement | Passage | Answer |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| X causes Y | Y causes X | FALSE |
| All birds migrate | Many birds migrate | FALSE |
| Plan was adopted | Plan was rejected | FALSE |
| Method is cheaper | Method is costlier | FALSE |

### 83.5 NOT GIVEN Conditions

Statement is NOT GIVEN when:

- Topic appears but **specific detail** in statement is absent.
- Passage is **silent** on comparison, degree, reason, or frequency asked in statement.
- Passage mentions related concept but not the **exact claim**.

**NG decision tree:**

1. Found relevant section? **No** → NG  
2. Found section — does it **confirm** statement? **Yes** → TRUE/YES  
3. Does it **contradict** statement? **Yes** → FALSE/NO  
4. Neither confirm nor contradict → **NG**

**Why NG is not "I didn't find it":** NG requires confirming the passage **does not address** the specific claim after thorough scan — not giving up after 10 seconds.

### 83.6 The Seven NG/FALSE Confusion Patterns

**Pattern 1 — Partial match**  
Passage discusses benefits; statement claims "main benefit is X." Passage lists benefits without ranking → **NG**.

**Pattern 2 — Different entity**  
Passage: "Company A expanded." Statement: "Company B expanded." → **FALSE** if B mentioned as not expanding; **NG** if B not mentioned at all.

**Pattern 3 — Time shift**  
Passage: past tense event. Statement: present/future without support → **NG** unless implied.

**Pattern 4 — Causal overreach**  
Passage: correlation mentioned. Statement: causation claimed → **NG** unless cause stated.

**Pattern 5 — Absolute language**  
Statement: always/never/all. Passage: often/rarely/most → **FALSE**.

**Pattern 6 — Reported speech vs fact**  
Passage quotes sceptic. Statement asserts sceptic's view as fact → TFNG **NG** or **FALSE** depending on writer's endorsement.

**Pattern 7 — Comparison without basis**  
Statement: "X is more efficient than Y." Passage discusses X efficiency only → **NG**.

### 83.7 TFNG vs YNNG: Side-by-Side

**Passage excerpt:** "It would be premature to conclude that artificial tutors replace human mentors entirely."

| Statement | TFNG | YNNG |
|-----------|------|------|
| Artificial tutors have replaced human mentors. | FALSE (premature to conclude replacement) | YES — writer implies full replacement has NOT occurred / is not yet valid; writer's view is that conclusion is premature → NO to "have replaced" |
| Some schools use artificial tutors. | NG if not mentioned | NG if not mentioned |
| Human mentors remain important. | NG unless stated | YES if "premature to conclude replacement" implies humans still necessary |

**Rule:** YNNG tracks **evaluative language** — `premature`, `unfortunately`, `surprisingly`, `should`, `must`, `it is clear that`.

### 83.8 Order and Location

TFNG/YNNG question sets usually follow **passage order** — statement 1 early in text, statement 7 later. Use this to bound scanning.

**Exception:** Occasional out-of-order item — do not force fit; if expected zone fails, expand search once then NG.

### 83.9 Frequency in the Test

Typically **5–7 TFNG** and/or **3–5 YNNG** per full Reading test. Combined, they often represent **25–30%** of questions — mastery here yields disproportionate band gains.

## Examples

### Example 1: TRUE

**Passage:** "Honeybees orient using polarised light patterns."  
**Statement:** "Bees navigate with the aid of polarised light."  
**Answer:** TRUE — orient using = navigate with aid of; paraphrase match.

### Example 2: FALSE

**Passage:** "The treaty came into force in 1992."  
**Statement:** "The agreement was implemented before 1990."  
**Answer:** FALSE — 1992 contradicts before 1990.

### Example 3: NOT GIVEN

**Passage:** "Solar installations grew rapidly in Spain."  
**Statement:** "Solar installations grew faster in Spain than in Portugal."  
**Answer:** NOT GIVEN — Spain's growth stated; Portugal not compared.

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Qualifier Trap

**Passage:** "Most respondents favoured remote work at least part-time."  
**Statement:** "All respondents preferred working from home."  
**Decomposition:** all vs most; preferred vs favoured part-time  
**Answer:** FALSE — quantifier and scope both contradicted.

### Worked Example 2: TFNG vs YNNG

**Passage:** "While advocates celebrate the reform as overdue, the data presented here suggests gains have been uneven across regions."

**Statement A:** "Reform gains have been uneven." → TFNG: **TRUE** (data suggests uneven gains)  
**Statement B:** "The writer celebrates the reform." → YNNG: **NO** (writer reports advocates celebrate; writer's own view emphasises uneven gains — not celebration)

### Worked Example 3: NG Discipline

**Passage:** "The excavation uncovered pottery shards dating to the fourth century."

**Statement:** "The excavation was led by Professor Nabil."  
**Scan:** Professor Nabil not mentioned anywhere.  
**Answer:** NOT GIVEN — not FALSE (passage doesn't say someone else led; silence on leadership)

## Exercises / Passages

### Passage: Bioluminescence in Marine Environments

Bioluminescence—the production of light by living organisms—is widespread among deep-sea species where sunlight never penetrates. Researchers once assumed that glowing served primarily to attract mates, but subsequent observations have documented at least four distinct functions, including predator confusion and cooperative hunting signals.

The chemical mechanism relies on luciferin oxidising in the presence of luciferase, releasing energy as photons. While terrestrial fireflies employ a similar biochemical pathway, marine variants often operate at near-freezing temperatures, requiring adapted enzymes that remain catalytically active under pressure exceeding two hundred atmospheres.

Commercial applications remain speculative. A Norwegian firm has experimented with bioluminescent markers for fishing gear to reduce seabird bycatch, yet trials published in 2021 showed only a modest reduction and raised concerns about ecological disturbance. Several commentators have urged caution before scaling such technologies, though the passage author notes that peer-reviewed long-term studies are still scarce.

**Questions 1–10**

*Questions 1–6: TFNG*

1. Bioluminescence occurs only in deep-sea organisms.
2. Scientists originally believed bioluminescence was mainly related to reproduction.
3. Fireflies and marine organisms use identical enzymes for light production.
4. Bioluminescent fishing gear has eliminated seabird bycatch.
5. The 2021 trials demonstrated significant ecological harm.
6. Long-term peer-reviewed studies of commercial markers are widely available.

*Questions 7–10: YNNG (writer's views)*

7. The writer believes attracting mates is the sole function of bioluminescence.
8. The writer considers commercial deployment of bioluminescent markers premature without more evidence.
9. The writer argues that terrestrial bioluminescence is scientifically unimportant.
10. The writer is optimistic that adapted enzymes will soon power household lighting.

### Exercise Set B — Quick Logic (11–20)

Classify without passage: what answer type is most likely?

11. Statement adds "only" not in original concept.
12. Statement reverses cause and effect from passage.
13. Passage silent on topic of statement entirely.
14. Statement uses "might"; passage says "does."
15. Writer quotes critic; statement attributes quote to writer.

---

## Solutions

**1.** **FALSE** — "widespread among deep-sea" does not mean **only** deep-sea; fireflies (terrestrial) mentioned elsewhere in passage context of biochemistry.

**2.** **TRUE** — "Researchers once assumed that glowing served **primarily** to attract mates" = originally believed mainly reproduction.

**3.** **FALSE** — "similar biochemical pathway" but marine variants require "**adapted enzymes**" — not identical.

**4.** **FALSE** — "only a **modest reduction**" contradicts **eliminated**.

**5.** **NOT GIVEN** — trials "raised **concerns** about ecological disturbance" but did not **demonstrate significant harm** — concerns ≠ proven harm.

**6.** **FALSE** — Author notes long-term peer-reviewed studies are "**still scarce**" — contradicts "widely available."

**7.** **NO** — Writer states at least **four distinct functions**; mate attraction is one historical assumption, not writer's sole view.

**8.** **YES** — Commentators urge caution; author notes scarce long-term studies — aligns with caution before scaling; writer presents evidence supporting premature concern.

**9.** **NOT GIVEN** — Fireflies mentioned for comparison; writer never evaluates terrestrial importance.

**10.** **NOT GIVEN** — Enzymes discussed for marine context; household lighting never mentioned.

**11.** Likely FALSE (absolute "only")  
**12.** FALSE  
**13.** NOT GIVEN  
**14.** FALSE or TRUE depending on modal shift — "might" weaker than "does"  
**15.** YNNG often NO or NG — rarely YES unless writer agrees

## Review

- **TFNG** = factual match; **YNNG** = writer's view match.
- Protocol: decompose → locate → compare → qualifier check → NG test.
- **FALSE/NO** = contradiction; **NG** = no confirmation AND no contradiction on **specific claim**.
- Absolutes (`all`, `never`, `only`) vs hedges (`some`, `often`) → usually **FALSE**.
- Outside knowledge **invalid**.
- Questions mostly follow **passage order**.

## Exam Tips

1. **Underline qualifiers** in statements before scanning.
2. **NG is not default** — earn NG by confirming absence after scan.
3. **FALSE is underused** — if passage says the opposite, choose FALSE confidently.
4. **YNNG:** find writer's evaluative verbs and adverbs first.
5. **One element rule:** if ANY part of statement unsupported and none contradicted → NG.
6. **Do not combine sentences** from different paragraphs to justify TRUE.
7. **Reported speech** — attribute to speaker unless writer agrees.
8. **Chronology:** dates must align exactly for TRUE.
9. **Write full word** TRUE or FALSE as instructed — check centre convention.
10. **Review NG items last** — they take longest; secure TRUE/FALSE first.

## Band 9 Strategies

1. **Instant qualifier flagging:** Band 9 readers circle `all`, `never`, `primary`, `only` before reading passage — predicts FALSE in 80% of cases.

2. **Writer voice isolation:** In YNNG, mentally strikethrough all reported speech and citations; evaluate only authorial sentences.

3. **Minimal evidence rule:** One precise sentence sufficient for TRUE/FALSE — no accumulation of weak hints.

4. **NG stinginess:** Band 9 candidates mark NG only when they can state **what exact element** is missing ("comparison with Portugal," "leadership of excavation").

5. **Parallel statement batching:** Read statements 1–3 before scanning; scan once for cluster — reduces repeated scanning.

6. **Modal logic:** `may`, `could`, ` suggests` in passage cannot support `will`, `must`, `proves` in statement → FALSE or NG.

7. **Distinguish absence from denial:** Silence → NG; explicit denial → FALSE — never conflate.

8. **Post-mortem taxonomy:** Classify errors as qualifier, paraphrase, NG/FALSE confusion, or location — one drill per category weekly.

9. **YNNG from conclusion paragraphs:** Writer views often crystallise in final 2 paragraphs — weighted scan.

10. **Simulate hardest item first:** Practice Cambridge Passage 3 TFNG sets until NG/FALSE accuracy exceeds 90% untimed.
