## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Distinguish summary completion, note completion, table completion, and sentence completion formats.
- Predict grammatical form and semantic category of gaps before scanning the passage.
- Apply word-limit rules precisely, including hyphenation and number conventions.
- Locate gap answers using summary structure as a sequential guide.
- Handle summaries with and without option boxes (word lists).
- Avoid answers that fit grammar but fail meaning or exceed word limits.
- Copy spelling correctly from the passage when permitted and spell from memory when not.

## Theory / Explanation

### 87.1 Completion Question Family

All completion types share core logic: **fill a gap** using words from the passage (or from an option list) obeying **word limit**.

| Type | Presentation | Special feature |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Sentence completion** | Incomplete sentences below passage | Usually in order |
| **Summary completion** | Coherent paragraph summarising passage | May have title |
| **Note completion** | Bullet notes | Abbreviations sometimes acceptable |
| **Table completion** | Rows/columns | Category alignment critical |
| **Flow-chart completion** | Boxes + arrows | Sequential process logic |

**Word limit variants:** ONE WORD ONLY | NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS | NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER

**Articles:** "a/an/the" count as words when limit applies.

### 87.2 The Grammar-First Protocol

Before scanning, for each gap:

**Step 1 — Predict part of speech**  
Noun? Verb? Adjective? Number?

**Step 2 — Predict semantic field**  
Person, place, material, date, quantity, process stage?

**Step 3 — Read surrounding summary sentences**  
Summary paraphrases passage — adjacent gaps provide context.

**Step 4 — Scan passage at predicted zone**  
Sequential order usually holds.

**Step 5 — Verify fit**  
Insert answer — grammar + meaning + word limit.

**Why grammar-first:** Scanning without prediction lands wrong word class ("develop" when noun "development" needed).

### 87.3 Word Limit Rules (Official Conventions)

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Hyphenated compounds | May count as one word ("high-pressure") |
| Numbers | "40" or "forty" — both acceptable unless specified |
| Dates | "1998" acceptable as number |
| Spelling | Must be correct — British or American if both exist in English |
| Copy from text | When allowed, copy exact spelling from passage |
| Option box | Each word used once unless stated otherwise |

**Penalty:** Exceed word limit → **zero marks**, even if concept correct.

**Example:** Limit ONE WORD — answer "carbon dioxide" (two words) → wrong; accept "CO2" if in passage as single token.

### 87.4 With vs Without Option Box

**Without box (from passage):**  
Greater paraphrase distance; must extract exact word(s) meeting limit.

**With box (list of 10–14 words):**  
Eliminate by grammar + meaning; cross out used words if once only.

**Box strategy:**

1. Label each option's part of speech.
2. Fill obvious gaps first (unique grammar slots).
3. Eliminate remaining by meaning.

### 87.5 Sentence Completion Specifics

**Format:** Complete sentences using words from passage.

**Order:** Follows passage order predominantly.

**Grammar link:** Completed sentence must be grammatical — test by reading aloud.

**Trap:** Passage uses plural; sentence structure requires singular — adjust if limit allows exact form from text only (usually must match text form).

### 87.6 Summary Completion Specifics

Summary **compresses** passage — higher paraphrase density than sentence completion.

**Technique:** Treat summary as **mini-passage** — read entire summary first for narrative arc, then fill gaps sequentially.

**Title clue:** Summary title indicates theme — narrows scan zone for first gap.

### 87.7 Synonym Bridging

Summary rarely copies passage wording:

| Passage | Summary gap context | Answer from passage |
|---------|---------------------|---------------------|
| "via fermentation" | "through the process of ___" | fermentation |
| "demographic shift" | "change in population ___" | structure? composition? — scan for exact word |

Always extract **exact passage word** meeting limit, not your paraphrase.

### 87.8 Numbers and Proper Nouns

**Numbers:** Digit or word form usually both accepted.

**Proper nouns:** Capitalisation as in passage preferred.

**Acronyms:** If passage uses "NASA," do not expand unless passage expands.

### 87.9 Common Errors

| Error | Consequence |
|-------|-------------|
| Exceed word limit | Zero marks |
| Wrong word class | Zero marks |
| Correct concept, wrong spelling | Zero marks |
| Word not in passage (when required) | Zero marks |
| Grammar fits, meaning wrong | Zero marks |

## Examples

### Example 1: Grammar Prediction

**Summary:** "The team measured levels of ___ in groundwater samples."

Prediction: noun (chemical/substance name). Scan for measurement + groundwater.

**Passage:** "...concentrations of **nitrate** in aquifer wells..."

Answer: **nitrate** (ONE WORD)

### Example 2: Two-Word Limit

**Gap:** "a ___ layer"

Passage: "thin insulating membrane"

Answer: **thin insulating** (TWO WORDS) or if ONE WORD limit → **membrane** only if sentence allows.

### Example 3: Option Box Elimination

Options: accelerate, diversity, hydraulic, permanent, sediment...

**Gap:** "The ___ pressure of water cracked the dam."

Only **hydraulic** fits collocation → **hydraulic**

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Full Summary Gap

**Summary:** "Early railways reduced the ___ of raw materials to factories."

**Passage:** "Rail networks lowered **transport costs** for bulk goods."

Limit: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS.

**Answer:** transport costs — paraphrase: reduced = lowered; raw materials to factories = bulk goods.

### Worked Example 2: Sentence Completion

**Sentence:** "Researchers used ___ to track nocturnal migration."

**Passage:** "...attached **radio transmitters** to individuals..."

Limit: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS → **radio transmitters**

### Worked Example 3: Word Limit Trap

**Gap:** "warming"

Candidate wrote: "global warming" — limit ONE WORD → **wrong**  
Correct: **warming** if sentence reads "effects of ___" matching passage term, or find single word in text.

Fireflies produce light through a reaction between luciferin and the enzyme luciferase in specialised abdominal cells. Oxygen availability governs flash frequency, allowing males to broadcast species-specific patterns recognisable to females. Synthetic luciferase variants have been engineered for laboratory assays detecting bacterial contamination, though production costs remain prohibitive for widespread field deployment. Field biologists emphasise that light pollution disrupts mating signals, potentially accelerating population decline in urban peripheries.

**Complete the summary below. Choose NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage for each answer.**

**Sentence completion (ONE WORD ONLY):**

5. Males produce flash patterns that are specific to their ___.
6. Urban ___ may harm firefly numbers at city edges.

## Exam Tips

1. **Write legibly** on answer sheet — illegible = wrong.
2. **Transfer as you go** — don't lose answers.
3. **Check every gap** against limit in Pass 3.
4. **Box list:** fill confident gaps first to eliminate options.
5. **Singular/plural** must match sentence grammar.
6. **Numbers:** consistent form not required between digit/word.
7. **Don't add articles** not in passage if limit tight.
8. **Summary title** — read it first.
9. **Adjacent gaps** — sequential scan zone.
10. **Listening completion** (Ch 76) — same word-limit discipline.

## Band 9 Strategies

1. **Parallel summary read:** read summary and locate first gap zone in one coordinated pass.

2. **Collocation lock:** adjective + noun gaps resolved by collocation patterns (Chapter 70).

3. **No conceptual answering** — Band 9 never writes synonym not in text when extraction required.

4. **Limit check last** — verify every gap in Pass 3 with finger on each word counting.

5. **Box elimination cascade:** one unique grammatical slot can eliminate 8 options instantly.

6. **Number + unit:** if gap expects number, confirm unit not extra word breaking limit.

7. **Summary discourse markers** — `however`, `therefore` show gap logic relation.

8. **Minimal answer length** — prefer shorter valid answer within limit (less spelling risk).

9. **Cross-check sentence completion** by reading completed sentence only — catches grammar misfits.

10. **AWL noun forms** (Chapter 69) — summary gaps often AWL nouns: implementation, distribution, mechanism.

### 87.10 Table Completion

Tables organise information by **row/column category**.

**Method:**

1. Read column headers — predict content type per column.
2. Read row labels — constrain semantic field.
3. Scan passage section matching table topic.
4. Verify cell answer aligns with **both** row and column.

**Example table:**

| Year | Event | Location |
|------|-------|----------|
| 2008 | ? | British Columbia |

Scan for 2008 + event + location cluster.

### 87.11 Note Completion vs Summary

**Notes:** telegraphic — noun phrases, bullet points; abbreviations sometimes acceptable if in passage.

**Summary:** full sentences — grammar must flow.

Same extraction logic; notes tolerate fragments more.

### 87.12 Controlled Vocabulary Box — Advanced Elimination

When 12 options for 6 gaps:

- Tag options: 4 nouns, 3 verbs, 3 adjectives, 2 adverbs.
- Gaps with "was ___" before noun → adjective slot eliminates 9 options instantly.

## Additional Passage Exercise

### Passage: Photic Zone Adaptation

Marine species inhabiting the photic zone—the ocean layer receiving sufficient sunlight for photosynthesis—display distinct visual adaptations. Many pelagic fish possess tubular eyes oriented upward to detect silhouettes against brighter surface waters while minimising exposure of reflective tissue to predators below. Counter-illumination via bioluminescent ventral photophores helps mesopelagic organisms match downwelling light intensity, reducing conspicuousness. Colour vision varies: surface dwellers often retain trichromatic capability, whereas deep-transition species may lose red-sensitive opsins as red wavelengths attenuate within metres.

**Summary completion (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS):**

Species in the photic zone show specialised (1) ___ adaptations. Pelagic fish use (2) ___ eyes to spot shapes above. Some animals employ (3) ___ to match light from above. Red-sensitive opsins may disappear as (4) ___ wavelengths fade rapidly.

**Solutions with WHY:**

1. **visual** — opening sentence "distinct **visual** adaptations"  
2. **tubular** — "possess **tubular** eyes"  
3. **counter-illumination** (or bioluminescent photophores — TWO WORDS: **counter-illumination**)  
4. **red** — "**red** wavelengths attenuate"

## Additional Passage 2: Microplastics in Freshwater

Freshwater systems receive microplastics from wastewater effluent, tyre wear particles transported by storm runoff, and atmospheric deposition. Filtration plants remove variable percentages depending on fibre size; particles below ten micrometres often pass through conventional treatment. Fish ingestion studies document inflammatory responses and reduced feeding rates, though population-level collapse has not been uniformly observed. Policy responses include upgrading treatment tiers and banning intentionally added microbeads in cosmetics—a measure already enacted in several jurisdictions.

**Summary (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS):**

Microplastics enter rivers via wastewater, tyre wear, and (1) ___. Conventional treatment fails to catch particles below (2) ___ micrometres. Cosmetics bans target intentionally added (3) ___.

**Sentence completion (ONE WORD):** Fish may show reduced ___ after ingesting particles.

**Solutions:** 1. **atmospheric deposition** (TWO WORDS) or **deposition** | 2. **ten** | 3. **microbeads** | Sentence: **feeding**

## Summary Completion Without Box — Step Sequence

1. Read summary title + full summary (60 sec)  
2. Predict each gap: POS + semantic field  
3. Scan passage in summary order  
4. Write exact words; count limits  
5. Re-read completed summary for coherence  
6. Transfer to answer sheet; verify spelling  

Repeat until automatic under 8 minutes per 6-gap summary.
