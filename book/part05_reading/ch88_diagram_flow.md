## Theory / Explanation

### 88.1 What Visual Completion Tests

Diagram and flow-chart completion appear primarily in **Academic Reading**, often in passages describing:

- Natural processes (water cycle, rock formation)
- Manufacturing or technical procedures
- Biological mechanisms (photosynthesis, digestion)
- Organisational or historical sequences

You fill **labels** on diagrams or **boxes** in flow-charts using words from the passage (with word limits).

**Skills tested:**

- **Sequential comprehension** — stage A before stage B
- **Visual-spatial translation** — convert prose to diagram structure
- **Precise terminology** — technical nouns and verbs
- **Directionality** — distinguish input/output, cause/effect, loop-back

### 88.2 Flow-Chart Structure

Typical flow-chart:

```
[Stage 1: ______] → [Stage 2: ______] → [Stage 3: ______]
                        ↓
                   [Branch: ______]
```

**Reading the chart before the passage:**

1. Count number of gaps.
2. Note arrow direction — linear vs branching vs cyclical.
3. Read any fixed text in boxes (anchors for scanning).
4. Predict process type (natural, industrial, administrative).

### 88.3 Process Language Signposts

Passages describe processes using **sequential markers**:

| Marker type | Examples |
|-------------|----------|
| First stage | first, initially, begins with, the process commences |
| Middle | subsequently, then, following this, once X is complete |
| Final | finally, ultimately, the end product |
| Conditional | when, after, before, as soon as |
| Parallel | simultaneously, at the same time (watch — may not be sequential on chart) |
| Loop | returns to, cycles back, is reused |

**Why signposts matter:** Passage may **not** describe stages in diagram order — you must **resequence** prose into chart order.

### 88.4 Diagram Label Completion

**Format:** Picture (or description) of object/system with numbered gaps pointing to parts.

**Strategy:**

1. Study diagram — what does each gap point to physically?
2. Scan passage for **descriptive section** listing components.
3. Match physical location to terminology (e.g., gap pointing to top chamber → "upper reservoir").

**Trap:** Passage mentions same component in different context — match diagram pointer, not first mention.

### 88.5 Word Limits and Technical Terms

Same rules as Chapter 87:

- ONE WORD / TWO WORDS / NUMBER
- Copy spelling from passage
- Hyphenated technical terms may count as one word

**Technical passages:** Answers often **low-frequency nouns** — precise scanning required.

### 88.6 Non-Linear Processes

Some charts include:

- **Feedback loops** — output returns as input  
- **Branching** — if condition A, path 1; else path 2  
- **Simultaneous steps** — two boxes same level  

**Method:** Draw quick sketch on question paper mirroring chart; annotate passage stages beside boxes as you find them.

### 88.7 Relationship to Listening Diagram Tasks

| Feature | Reading | Listening |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Input | Written passage | Audio |
| Re-read allowed | Yes | No |
| Spelling | From text visible | Often heard once |
| Time pressure | Shared with all questions | Per section |

Reading advantage: **re-scan** for exact spelling. Use it.

### 88.8 Locating Process Passages

Process descriptions often occupy **central paragraphs** after introduction. Look for:

- Passive voice sequences ("is heated," "is filtered")
- Imperative in generic descriptions ("The mixture is then cooled")
- Numbered stages in passage (rare but helpful)

### 88.9 Common Errors

| Error | Fix |
|-------|-----|
| Wrong stage order | Track signposts, not passage paragraph order |
| Wrong word class | Grammar prediction (Ch 87) |
| Synonym instead of passage word | Extract exact term |
| Missing branch condition | Read arrows showing splits |
| Overwriting allowed words | Respect word limit |

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Resequencing Prose

**Passage order:** Paragraph explains packaging, then heating, then harvesting.

**Chart order:** Harvest → Heat → Package

**Method:** Ignore paragraph order; extract stages by signposts:

- "Upon **harvest**, beans are dried"
- "**Roasting** follows at 200°C"
- "**Vacuum sealing** extends shelf life"

### Worked Example 2: Diagram Part Label

**Gap 3** points to lower turbine chamber.

**Passage:** "...water strikes **pelton wheels** housed in the **lower casing**..."

If gap points to wheels → pelton wheels; if casing → lower casing. **Diagram pointer decides.**

### Worked Example 3: Word Limit

Limit ONE WORD. Passage says "rapid cooling phase."

Chart box: "Stage 2: ___"

Answer: **cooling** (not "rapid cooling" if TWO WORDS not allowed and "cooling" alone in passage)

## Exercises / Passages

### Passage: The Manufacture of Recycled Paper

Collected paper arrives at the mill and is sorted to remove contaminants such as staples and plastic film. The cleaned pulp undergoes shredding before being mixed with water in large vats to form a slurry. Chemical agents break down ink particles in a process called de-inking. The slurry is then spread onto mesh screens where water drains away, leaving a thin mat of fibres. Rollers press and heat this mat into continuous sheets, which are finally trimmed to standard dimensions.

**Complete the flow-chart below. NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage.**

```
Paper collected → Sorting/removal of contaminants → (1) ______
       ↓
Mixed with water → (2) ______ formed → De-inking removes ink
       ↓
Spread on mesh → Water drains → (3) ______ of fibres remains
       ↓
(4) ______ and heat → Continuous sheets → Trimming
```

**Diagram label (ONE WORD ONLY):**

5. The chemical process that removes ink is called ___.

### Exercise Set B (6–8)

6. Flow-chart arrows indicate?
7. Passage order ≠ chart order — action?
8. Diagram gap points to part — decide by?

## Solutions

**1.** **shredding** — "undergoes shredding" (or "cleaned pulp" if gap includes prior — context: after sorting, shredding comes next)

**2.** **slurry** — "form a slurry"

**3.** **thin mat** — "thin mat of fibres" (TWO WORDS)

**4.** **Rollers press** — "Rollers press and heat" → **rollers** or **pressing** — ONE WORD if limit: **rollers**; TWO WORDS: **rollers press**

*For TWO WORD limit gap 4: **rollers press** or **press rollers** — passage: "Rollers press and heat" → **rollers press***

**5.** **de-inking** — hyphenated one word

**6.** Sequence and direction of stages  
**7.** Resequence using signposts  
**8. Physical pointer on diagram**, not first passage mention

- **Resequence** if prose order ≠ chart order.
- Same **word limits** as other completion types.
- **Exact passage words**; copy spelling.
- Diagram: gap pointer determines answer, not keyword proximity.

## Exam Tips

1. **Sketch mini-flow** on question paper while reading.
2. **Fixed chart text** = scan anchors.
3. **Don't invent stages** not in passage.
4. **Branch labels** — both paths need answers from text.
5. **Copy technical spelling** — de-inking, homogenisation.
6. **Verify stage count** — gaps match stages found.
7. **Passive sequences** — common in process paragraphs.
8. **Time box 6 minutes** — return if stuck.
9. **Adjacent boxes** — sequential scan in passage.
10. **Task 1 process writing** (Ch 100) — reciprocal skill; study both.

## Band 9 Strategies

1. **Chart-first mandatory** — Band 9 never reads process passage without chart visible.

2. **Signpost annotation:** mark 1,2,3 in passage margin matching chart boxes in first read.

3. **Branch precision:** conditional stages — both branches verified against "if/whether" clauses.

4. **Loop identification:** cyclical processes — last box often repeats first stage vocabulary.

5. **No stage invention** — Band 9 rejects grammar-plausible but text-absent words.

6. **Parallel box handling:** simultaneous steps filled from single sentence listing both.

7. **Minimal re-read:** one intensive scan of process zone sufficient with good annotation.

8. **Cross-link Writing Task 1:** process diagram vocabulary shared — study together.

9. **Spelling harvest:** note unusual spellings when first scanning — copy exactly.

10. **Time cap 5 min** for typical 4-gap flow-chart — move on decisively.

### 88.10 Cycle Diagrams

Cycles return to starting point: water cycle, carbon cycle, life cycle.

**Method:** Identify **all stages** in passage order around loop; first and last box may share vocabulary (evaporation appears at start and end).

**Trap:** Linear reading misses final loop-back stage.

### 88.11 Map and Plan Labelling (Reading Variant)

Occasionally Reading includes **plan/map** with lettered rooms or areas — same as Listening map logic but text-based description.

**Strategy:** Orient map north/up; scan description walking through space sequentially ("turn left," "adjacent to").

### 88.12 Classification Charts

Tables with **process stage × material type** — fill cell where row and column intersect in passage.

## Additional Passage: Cement Production

Cement manufacture begins with quarrying limestone and clay, which are crushed and blended into raw meal. The meal enters a rotating kiln heated to approximately 1450°C, triggering clinker formation. After cooling, clinker is ground with gypsum to control setting time. The finished powder is bagged or loaded for transport. Emissions from kilns motivate industry trials substituting calcined clay and recycled materials for a portion of clinker, reducing carbon intensity.

**Flow-chart (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS):**

(1) Quarry limestone/clay → (2) ___ and blend → Kiln at 1450°C → (3) ___ forms → Cool → Grind with (4) ___ → Finished cement

**Diagram label (ONE WORD):** The rotating oven is called a ___.

**Solutions:** 1. (implicit quarry — skip if given) 2. **crushed** (crushed and blended) 3. **clinker** 4. **gypsum** | Diagram: **kiln**

**TFNG on same passage:**

5. Gypsum speeds up setting indefinitely — FALSE (controls setting time, not indefinite speed)  
6. Substituting materials can lower carbon intensity — TRUE (trials reducing carbon intensity)

## Process Signpost Master List

Memorise these clusters for rapid flow-chart construction:

**Start:** begins, commences, first stage, initially  
**Middle:** then, next, subsequently, once X is complete, following  
**Branch:** if/whether, in cases where, alternatively  
**Loop:** returns to, cycles back, is reused, re-enters  
**End:** finally, ultimately, the end product, results in  
**Simultaneous:** while, at the same time, simultaneously  

Drill: highlight all signposts in one process passage per study session.

## Additional Passage 2: Photosynthesis Overview

Light-dependent reactions occur in thylakoid membranes, splitting water to release oxygen and transfer electrons to carrier molecules. ATP and NADPH produced thereby fuel the Calvin cycle in the stroma, where carbon dioxide fixation builds three-carbon sugars. Sugars may assemble into starch for storage or feed cellular respiration. When light intensity saturates, photorespiration can reduce efficiency in C3 plants; C4 and CAM pathways evolved biochemical concentrating mechanisms mitigating this loss in hot or arid environments.

**Flow-chart gaps (ONE WORD where possible):**

Light hits thylakoid → water split → (1) ___ released → ATP/NADPH formed → (2) ___ cycle fixes CO₂ → sugars → (3) ___ or respiration

**Solutions:** 1. **oxygen** | 2. **Calvin** | 3. **starch**

When labels numbered 1–5 on diagram:

- Scan passage for **enumerated description** matching order

## Full Worked Example: Water Treatment Plant

**Passage excerpt:** Raw water enters coagulation tanks where alum causes particles to clump. Flocs settle in sedimentation basins before sand filtration removes remaining solids. Chlorine disinfection precedes distribution to households.

**Flow-chart:**

Raw water → (1) ___ added → particles clump → (2) ___ basins → sand filtration → (3) ___ → distribution
