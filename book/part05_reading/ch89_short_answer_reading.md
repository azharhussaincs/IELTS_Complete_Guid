## Theory / Explanation

### 89.1 Format Overview

Short-answer questions ask you to respond to **direct questions** such as:

- What did researchers discover in 2019?
- Who funded the initial expedition?
- How many species were catalogued?
- Where was the prototype assembled?

**Typical instruction:** "Answer the questions below. Choose **NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER** from the passage for each answer."

**Marks:** 1 per correct answer. Usually **3–5 questions** per set, occasionally more.

### 89.2 Question Word → Scan Type

| Question word | Scan target | Answer type |
|---------------|-------------|-------------|
| **What** | Thing, concept, process name | Noun phrase |
| **Who** | Person, group, organisation | Proper noun / role |
| **When** | Date, time, period | Number / proper noun |
| **Where** | Place | Proper noun / location |
| **How many/much** | Quantity | Number (+ optional unit) |
| **Which** | Specific choice from implied set | Noun / adjective |
| **Why** | Reason (if short answer — rare) | Short phrase from text |

**Why question word matters:** "What" questions rarely answered by dates; "How many" must include number from text.

### 89.3 Precision Rules

**Answer must be:**

- **In passage** (exact words — not synonym you invent)
- **Within word limit**
- **Correct spelling**
- **Relevant to question** — not nearby interesting fact

**Incomplete answer trap:**

Question: "What material was used in the filter?"  
Passage: "a **charcoal filter**"  
Wrong: **filter** (incomplete)  
Correct: **charcoal** or **charcoal filter** (per limit)

### 89.4 Numbers and Units

**How many / How much:**

- Include number: **17**, **seventeen**, **10,000**
- Units: if limit allows — "**50 kilometres**" (two words + number rules vary — check instruction)

**Official flexibility:** Digit vs word form for numbers usually interchangeable.

**Dates:** **1998**, **March 2012** — count as number/date unit.

### 89.5 Order and Location

Short-answer questions **almost always follow passage order**. Use as scan bounds:

- Q8 answer between Q7 and Q9 locations in text.

### 89.6 Why vs Short Answer

True **why** questions seeking explanations may exceed short format — IELTS usually expects **short phrase** from passage:

Question: "Why did the programme end?"  
Passage: "...**funding was withdrawn** in April."  
Answer: **funding was withdrawn** (up to limit) or **withdrawn funding** if grammar fits limit — prefer contiguous phrase from text.

**Do not paraphrase** reason in your own words unless it matches passage lexis.

### 89.7 Overlap with Sentence Completion

Some tests blur formats — incomplete sentences vs direct questions. **Same protocol:** grammar fit + word limit + passage extraction.

### 89.8 Plural and Article Handling

If limit ONE WORD and answer is "**policies**" (plural in text), write plural.

Do not add "**the**" unless in passage and within limit.

### 89.9 Common Errors

| Error | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Too many words | "the city of Manchester" (4 words) when limit 2 |
| Wrong question focus | Who → answered with place |
| Inference beyond text | Logical but not stated |
| Spelling error | "luciferin" misspelled |
| Synonym substitution | "funds" when text says "grants" |

## Review

- Question word dictates **answer category**.
- Answers = **exact passage extract** within limit.
- **Order follows passage** — sequential scan.
- Numbers/dates flexible form; spelling must be correct.
- Avoid **incomplete** or **over-long** answers.
- Rarely require inference beyond stated text.

## Exam Tips

1. **Circle question word** before scanning.
2. **Copy numbers** carefully — decimal points matter.
3. **Check word count** on answer sheet in Pass 3.
4. **Contiguous phrase** from text — don't assemble disjoint words.
5. **Who** may be organisation, not person.
6. **Which** implies limited set in passage — locate comparison.
7. **Don't answer next question's detail** for current question.
8. **Hyphenated words** — one word if hyphenated in text.
9. **Legible handwriting** on answer sheet.
10. **Listening short answer** (Ch 77) — similar limits, less re-read time.

## Band 9 Strategies

1. **Question batching:** read all short answers in set; single scan pass for all.

2. **Boundary respect:** never reuse answer location backwards in text.

3. **Minimum complete answer:** shortest phrase that fully satisfies question word.

4. **Number fidelity:** 1.1 million ≠ 1 million — copy exactly.

5. **No decoration:** Band 9 strips unnecessary adjectives unless in question scope.

6. **Who/Which disambiguation:** when multiple names present, match verb attribution precisely.

7. **Unit awareness:** km, °C, % — include if part of contiguous passage phrase within limit.

8. **Parallel with note completion** — same extraction logic, different layout.

9. **Instant limit check** after writing each answer.

10. **Integrate scan skills** Ch 82 — short answer is pure scan + extract discipline.

### 89.10 Which vs What vs Who

**Which** implies limited set in passage: "Which method..." — scan comparison section listing methods.

**What** broader — any acceptable answer meeting criteria.

**Who** — person or organisation; check verb attribution.

### 89.11 How Questions (Procedure)

**How** may ask mechanism or method:

"How was the sample purified?" → extract verb phrase: "**by centrifugation**" → limit may allow **centrifugation** (ONE WORD).

### 89.12 Answers as Dates and Proper Nouns

Dates not requiring prepositions: **1998** not "in 1998" unless limit allows two words.

Proper nouns: capitalisation as in passage.

## Additional Passage: CRISPR Gene Editing

CRISPR-Cas9 enables targeted DNA cuts guided by RNA sequences complementary to target loci. Doudna and Charpentier's 2012 paper catalysed explosive adoption in laboratories worldwide. Clinical trials now address sickle-cell disease and certain inherited blindnesses by editing haematopoietic stem cells **ex vivo** before reinfusion. Germline editing—modifying embryos—remains ethically restricted in most jurisdictions following the 2018 He Jiankui controversy. Off-target cuts, where Cas9 cleaves unintended sites, motivate improved base-editing variants with narrower activity windows.

**Short Answer (NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER):**

1. What system guides DNA cuts?  
2. Who co-authored the influential 2012 paper? (TWO names — THREE WORDS: **Doudna and Charpentier**)  
3. When was the pivotal paper published?  
4. What disease is mentioned for clinical trials? (accept **sickle-cell disease** or **sickle-cell**)  
5. What unwanted outcome involves unintended cleavage sites?  
6. What editing type modifies embryos?  

**Solutions with WHY:**

1. **CRISPR-Cas9** or **RNA sequences** — question asks guiding system → **RNA sequences** (complementary RNA guides cuts) or CRISPR-Cas9 as tool  
2. **Doudna and Charpentier** — named directly  
3. **2012** — "2012 paper"  
4. **sickle-cell disease** — explicitly listed  
5. **off-target cuts** — TWO WORDS  
6. **germline editing** — TWO WORDS

## Extended Worked Example 4: How Many Precision

**Q:** How many jurisdictions restrict germline editing?

**Passage:** "remains ethically restricted in **most** jurisdictions"

**Analysis:** "Most" is not a number — short answer requires number → if question expects number, answer may be **NOT GIVEN** in TFNG; short answer must be exact from text — "**most**" may not qualify as number answer. **Exam tip:** IELTS short answer with "how many" expects numeral — if only "most" given, question design may use different wording. This item teaches precision: **how many** ≠ **most**.

## Question Word Decision Tree

```
WHAT → thing/concept/process name
WHO → person/organisation (check verb)
WHEN → date/time/period
WHERE → place/location
HOW MANY/MUCH → number/quantity (exact from text)
WHICH → limited set — scan comparison
WHY → reason phrase from text (not invented)
HOW → method/mechanism — verb or noun phrase
```

Apply tree before every short-answer scan.

## Additional Passage 2: The Antikythera Mechanism

Recovered from a first-century BCE shipwreck, the Antikythera mechanism consists of bronze gears predicting astronomical positions and eclipse cycles. Computed tomography in 2006 revealed at least thirty-seven meshing gears, far exceeding complexity previously attributed to ancient Mediterranean craftsmanship. Researchers propose the device drew on Babylonian arithmetic combined with Greek geometric theories. No contemporary written manual survives; function reconstruction relies on legible inscriptions and gear tooth counts. The artifact suggests that analog computation existed centuries before medieval clockwork, revising assumptions about technological trajectories.

**Questions (NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER):**

7. What material forms the mechanism?  
8. How many gears did 2006 imaging reveal at minimum?  
9. What two cultural traditions may have influenced design? (pick one for THREE WORDS: **Babylonian arithmetic** or list as separate Q)  
10. What lacks from contemporaries documenting use?  
11. What does the artifact challenge about ancient technology?  

**Solutions:** 7. **bronze** | 8. **thirty-seven** / **37** | 9. **Babylonian arithmetic** (or Greek geometric — check question wording) | 10. **written manual** / **contemporary manual** | 11. **assumptions about technological trajectories** (THREE WORDS: **technological trajectories** if "what" — accept **analog computation** if question asks what it demonstrates)

## Institutional Short-Answer Patterns

IELTS repeatedly tests:

- **Research dates** (when study published)  
- **Researcher names** (who led team)  
- **Sample sizes** (how many participants)  
- **Location names** (where trial occurred)  
- **Material/substance** (what used)  
- **Measured outcome** (what was reduced/increased)  

When scanning, hunt these six categories first.

## Common Short-Answer Phrasing in Questions

| Question phrasing | Expect in passage |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| "What type of..." | category noun |
| "What part of..." | body part / section noun |
| "In which year..." | date |
| "What name is given to..." | defined term after "called/termed" |
| "What proportion..." | number/percentage |
| "Which group..." | collective noun |

Recognising phrasing pattern accelerates scan anchor selection by 10–15 seconds per item — meaningful across 40-question test.
