## The task
General Training Task 1 asks you to write a letter of at least 150 words in about 20 minutes. The prompt gives a situation and three bullet points. Address every bullet point fully, use a tone that matches the reader, and make the purpose clear from the first paragraph.

## Tone and format
Formal: *Dear Sir or Madam, ... Yours faithfully*. Semi-formal: *Dear Mr Khan, ... Yours sincerely*. Informal: *Dear Sam, ... Best wishes*. A formal complaint needs polite precision, not anger. A message to a friend can use contractions and warmth, but it still needs clear paragraphs.

## Four-part plan
Open with purpose and relevant context. Deal with the first and second bullet points in separate, developed paragraphs. Complete the final point and state an appropriate request, offer, or next action. Plan details before writing: who, what happened, when, impact, and desired outcome.

## Useful language
Formal requests: *I am writing to enquire about; I would be grateful if you could; I would appreciate a response by*. Complaints: *I wish to express my dissatisfaction; the item failed to match the description; I request a replacement or refund*. Informal language: *I thought I’d let you know; would you be able to; it would be great if*.

## Model: formal complaint
Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to complain about a bicycle I rented from your Riverside branch on 4 June. Although I booked it for a full day, its rear brake failed within twenty minutes of collection.

When I returned to the branch, the attendant acknowledged that the brake cable was worn but could not provide a replacement. Consequently, I had to cancel a planned trip with visiting relatives and pay for taxis instead. I was particularly disappointed because your website describes every bicycle as safety-checked.

I would therefore like a full refund of the rental fee and reimbursement for the taxi costs, for which I have enclosed receipts. Please confirm how you intend to resolve this matter within seven days.

Yours faithfully,
Alex Morgan

**Examiner comment:** The purpose, all bullet points, suitable formal register, and a clear requested action are developed. Notice that the tone is firm without being rude.

## Practice tasks
1. Write to a college director requesting a timetable change; explain the clash, propose an alternative, and ask about the decision process.
2. Write to a neighbour about a noisy renovation; describe the impact, suggest reasonable hours, and invite a reply.
3. Write to a friend who is moving city; give advice about housing, offer practical help, and arrange a meeting.
4. Write to a hotel after a disappointing stay; explain two problems, say what staff did, and request compensation.
