## How to use this chapter
Practice improves when it is diagnostic. Do not write ten reports and only count words. Before each task, predict the overview. After writing, mark every comparison, check every unit, and identify one recurring language error. Rewrite one paragraph rather than merely reading feedback.

## Academic practice set
1. A line graph shows the number of electric and petrol cars sold in three countries between 2012 and 2024.
2. A bar chart compares weekly hours spent on paid work, childcare, and leisure by men and women in 1995 and 2025.
3. Two pie charts show sources of electricity in a region in 2005 and 2025.
4. A table gives population, urban share, and average income in five cities.
5. Two maps show a library site before and after renovation.
6. A process diagram illustrates how discarded glass is recycled into new containers.
7. A combined bar and line chart shows advertising spend and online sales over six years.

## General Training practice set
1. Write to your manager to request training leave. Explain the course, how it benefits work, and how duties will be covered.
2. Write to a local council about a dangerous crossing. Describe the problem, explain who is affected, and suggest a solution.
3. Write to a friend who has offered accommodation. Thank them, explain your arrival plans, and ask one practical question.

## Review rubric
Task response: Is every required feature or bullet point developed? Organisation: Does each paragraph have a purpose? Vocabulary: Are words precise and natural? Grammar: Are complex sentences controlled? Make an error log with columns for error, correction, reason, and personal example.

## Timed cycle
Use three minutes to analyse, fourteen to write, and three to edit. On a second attempt, reduce planning to two minutes only after you can consistently identify the overview. Never cut editing time; a correction to subject–verb agreement or a data unit can protect several marks.
