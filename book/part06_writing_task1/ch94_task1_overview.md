## What Academic Task 1 assesses
In Academic Writing Task 1, you write at least 150 words in about 20 minutes to describe a graph, chart, table, map, or process. The task measures four public criteria: Task Achievement, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. It is not a test of your opinion. A report that explains reasons not shown in the visual can become less relevant even when its English is fluent.

## The core method: read, select, organise, verify
First, read the rubric and visual with forensic care. Identify the unit, time period, and categories. Second, select the dominant features: extremes, major changes, stable items, intersections, and striking contrasts. Third, organise related information rather than following a visual mechanically. Finally, verify that every figure, tense, and comparison is accurate.

## Structure that serves the reader
Use an introduction to paraphrase, a separate overview to communicate the main patterns, and two focused detail paragraphs. The overview is indispensable: it answers “what should a busy reader notice first?” A line graph may require trends; a bar chart may require rankings; a map may require the principal redevelopments; a process may require its start, finish, and linear or cyclical nature.

## Timing and language
Spend roughly three minutes planning, fourteen minutes drafting, and three minutes checking. Use precise reporting verbs: *stood at, accounted for, rose to, fell by, exceeded, remained stable*. Use comparison grammar: *while, whereas, compared with, respectively, twice as high as*. Do not manufacture complexity. One accurate complex sentence is better than three unstable ones.

## Error patterns
Do not omit the overview; list every number; use a conclusion that repeats it; report causes not supplied; confuse *rise to* with *rise by*; or describe a future projection as a past fact. Keep the report objective: “The figure increased” rather than “I think the figure increased.”

## Practice protocol
Collect ten authentic-style visuals. For each, make a 90-second plan, write an overview without numbers, and select six supporting data points. Then write under timed conditions. Review with a four-colour code: one colour for overview claims, one for figures, one for comparisons, and one for errors. If a body paragraph contains figures but no comparison, rewrite it.

## Mini model
The line graph compares annual visits to two museums from 2010 to 2020. Overall, attendance at the modern-art museum rose substantially, whereas visits to the history museum were broadly stable. Starting at 120,000, the former climbed to nearly 250,000 before easing slightly. The latter fluctuated between 140,000 and 155,000, so the initially smaller venue became the more popular one by the middle of the period.

## Final checklist
Have you met the word minimum; accurately paraphrased; supplied a real overview; grouped evidence; used the right tense; checked plurals, articles, spelling, and units; and avoided personal opinion? These habits make a report reliable at every band.
