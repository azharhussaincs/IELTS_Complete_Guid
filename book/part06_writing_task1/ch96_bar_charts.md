## Purpose and success criteria
Bar charts usually require clear comparison between discrete categories, places, or years. These tasks test your ability to select, organise, and compare visual information in at least 150 words. They do not reward specialist knowledge or imaginative explanation. A strong report gives a faithful overview, groups related features, and supports each comparison with accurate data. Aim to plan for three minutes, write for fourteen, and reserve three minutes to check units, tense, articles, and spelling.

## Reliable four-paragraph structure
1. **Introduction:** paraphrase the task in one sentence: chart type, subject, place, and period.
2. **Overview:** state two or three highest-level features. Do not load this paragraph with every number.
3. **Body paragraph 1:** present a logical group, usually the largest values, a shared trend, or the first time period.
4. **Body paragraph 2:** complete the remaining meaningful comparison.

The structure is not a formula for copying. Its purpose is to make the reader see your organisation. If a graphic has two strongly contrasting groups, compare them by group; if it has a clear chronology, compare it by stage.

## How to analyse before writing
Read the title, axes, legend, units, dates, and footnotes. Ask: What is measured? Which feature is highest or lowest? What changes most? Where do categories converge, diverge, overtake, or remain stable? Circle no more than six useful figures. An overview is a judgement about patterns, not a shortened data dump.

For a typical prompt, imagine that five age groups are compared for weekly exercise, with the 18–29 group leading and the oldest group lowest. Your notes might be: “first measure rises strongly; second remains near 30; widest gap in middle; first still leads at end.” These notes can become an overview. Exact figures belong in the body paragraphs.

## Vocabulary with control
Use words only when their meanings match the evidence.

**Comparisons:** exceed, outstrip, trail, be marginally higher than, be twice the figure for. **Ranking:** lead, rank first, occupy the lowest position. **Values:** stand at, record, register, account for.

For movement, use a verb plus an optional adverb: *rose gradually, fell sharply, remained broadly stable*. For a static comparison, prefer *stood at, accounted for, was twice as high as, exceeded, lagged behind*. Avoid “dramatically” for a change of one unit and avoid “respectively” unless the order is unmistakable.

## Sentence patterns
- X **stood at** 24%, compared with 18% for Y.
- X **rose from** 20 **to** 45, an increase of 25 units.
- While X increased, Y **remained broadly unchanged**.
- At 60%, A was **three times the proportion of** B.
- After peaking at 45, the figure **edged down** to 42.

Use past tense for completed periods, present tense for a chart with no time reference, and future language only for projections. Do not write “increased by 45” when the value rose *to* 45; that confuses change with final level.

## Common mistakes and repairs
**Listing:** “A was 20. B was 30. C was 40.” Repair it with a comparison: “A and B were lower than C, at 20 and 30 versus 40.”

**Missing overview:** A report can contain accurate details yet lose Task Achievement marks. Write the overview immediately after the introduction.

**Inventing explanations:** “This happened because people preferred…” is speculation. Report the visual, not an imagined cause.

**Mechanical grouping:** Chronological order is not always logical. Group values when that makes a trend clearer.

**Inaccurate approximation:** Use *about, roughly,* or *just over* only when the visual does not support precision. Never alter a clearly labelled number.

## Practice tasks
1. Write a 20-minute report on a graphic in which one category rises from 12% to 48%, two categories remain near 25%, and another falls from 35% to 18%.
2. Create an overview using no numbers for a chart with six categories. Then select five figures that prove it.
3. Rewrite a 170-word report that has no comparisons. Add contrast language without changing the facts.
4. Exchange a report with a partner and check every number against the source.

## Self-assessment checklist
Before submitting, ask: Have I described the visual rather than explained it? Is there a separate overview? Does each body paragraph have a grouping principle? Are all units and comparisons accurate? Have I used a range of precise language naturally? Are there at least 150 words, with no irrelevant conclusion?

### Band 6 sample answer
The graphic shows that five age groups are compared for weekly exercise, with the 18–29 group leading and the oldest group lowest. Overall, there was a clear difference between the main categories. The first measure was generally higher, although the pattern changed near the end. In the first period, the leading figure rose from 20 to 45, while the other value stayed close to 30. It then fell slightly. By the final period, the gap had become smaller. This answer is clear but reports several details separately and uses limited comparison.

The graphic shows that five age groups are compared for weekly exercise, with the 18–29 group leading and the oldest group lowest. ### Band 8 sample answer
The graphic shows that five age groups are compared for weekly exercise, with the 18–29 group leading and the oldest group lowest. Overall, one measure rose markedly to become the clear leader, while the other changed little; the most notable feature was the temporary widening of the gap. Beginning at 20, the first figure more than doubled to 45 before levelling off just below that point. By contrast, the second hovered at approximately 30 throughout. Thus, the series diverged sharply mid-period and remained separated at the close. The response groups information and uses data accurately.

### Band 9 sample answer
The graphic shows that five age groups are compared for weekly exercise, with the 18–29 group leading and the oldest group lowest. Overall, the principal measure expanded substantially, whereas the comparator was virtually static, producing a pronounced and enduring disparity after the midpoint. From a relatively modest base of 20, the former accelerated to 45, then consolidated at a marginally lower level. The latter, however, fluctuated only within a narrow band around 30. The salient pattern is therefore not simply growth, but the transformation from a small initial difference into a sustained lead. Every figure supports the overview rather than appearing as an isolated list.

**Why it earns this level:** Every figure supports the overview rather than appearing as an isolated list.

## Examiner perspective
At Band 6, a candidate usually addresses the task but may select details unevenly or make unclear comparisons. Band 7 reports the main features and organises them logically. Band 8 extends and supports key features with well-chosen detail. Band 9 is fully accurate, sharply selective, cohesive, and effortless to read. The decisive change is not ornate vocabulary; it is better decisions about what matters.
