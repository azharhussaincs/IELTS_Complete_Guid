## The task and marking
Write at least 250 words in about 40 minutes. Task Response assesses whether you answer every part and develop ideas; Coherence and Cohesion assesses organisation; Lexical Resource assesses precise, flexible vocabulary; Grammatical Range and Accuracy assesses controlled sentence variety. Task 2 carries more weight than Task 1, so manage time accordingly.

## A dependable workflow
Spend five minutes interpreting the question and planning. Write an introduction, two developed body paragraphs, and a conclusion in roughly thirty minutes. Reserve five minutes to check that your position is consistent, both parts are answered, and grammar is accurate. Two strong ideas are safer than four thin ones.

## The thesis test
Your thesis must answer the exact wording. For “discuss both views and give your opinion,” name both views and give your own judgement. For “what problems and what solutions,” give both. For a two-part question, do not let one paragraph become a general discussion while another question disappears.

## Development over decoration
Each body paragraph needs a claim, why it is true, an example, and why the example matters. Avoid memorised hooks, quotations, and imaginary statistics. Logical development produces cohesion: use a connector only when it names a real relationship.

## Editing priorities
Check subject–verb agreement, articles, singular/plural nouns, verb forms, pronoun reference, and punctuation around dependent clauses. Then replace one vague word with an exact one. Do not rewrite whole paragraphs in the final minutes.
