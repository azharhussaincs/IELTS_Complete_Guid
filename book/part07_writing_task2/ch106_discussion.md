## What the question requires
Represent both views fairly before making your own position unmistakable. The question is: **Some people think remote work benefits society, while others believe it damages communities. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.** Read it twice. Underline the task words, identify whether it asks for reasons, effects, solutions, comparison, evaluation, or your opinion, and make a one-sentence answer before planning examples. A sophisticated essay that answers a different question cannot score highly for Task Response.

## Flexible template
**Introduction:** paraphrase the issue and give a direct thesis.  
**Body paragraph 1:** topic sentence, explanation, specific example, and consequence.  
**Body paragraph 2:** a distinct main point developed in the same way; include a concession if it improves accuracy.  
**Conclusion:** restate the judgement or answer in fresh language.  

This is a planning scaffold, not text to memorise. Examiners recognise empty openings such as “This essay will discuss both sides.” Your thesis should contain meaning: “Although free tuition widens access, a targeted scheme is more sustainable than universal funding.”

## Planning in five minutes
Write P (position), A and B (two reasons), E (one realistic example for each), and C (a concession). Then test your ideas: can each be explained beyond one sentence? Is it directly relevant? Does the example illustrate rather than replace reasoning? Prefer a narrow, plausible example over an invented statistic.

## Paragraph architecture
A useful paragraph moves from claim to mechanism to illustration to implication. For example: “Targeted grants improve access because they remove the financial barrier for students from low-income households. A trainee nurse who can cover tuition and transport is less likely to abandon a course for paid work. This approach therefore broadens participation without subsidising affluent families who can already pay.” The example is specific, but no unverified number is needed.

## Linking without mechanical signals
Use logical relationships, not a chain of *firstly, secondly, finally*. Addition: *moreover, equally important*. Contrast: *however, nevertheless, whereas*. Cause: *therefore, consequently, which means that*. Concession: *admittedly, although, even if*. Reference words such as *this approach, such pressure,* and *the latter* can create cohesion when their referent is clear.

## Lexis and grammar
Favour precise collocations: *allocate public funds, widen access, impose a burden, mitigate a risk, foster independence*. Use complex grammar to express real relationships: “If employers subsidised retraining, displaced workers could move into growing sectors.” Accuracy matters more than density. Avoid absolute claims such as “technology always improves education.”

## Band 9 model
Some people think remote work benefits society, while others believe it damages communities. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

People often frame this issue as a choice between two simple alternatives. In reality, the strongest policy combines a clear principle with practical limits. I take the view that public action is justified when it removes a genuine barrier to opportunity, but it should be designed to concentrate help where it produces the greatest social return.

The principal reason is that unequal access can reproduce disadvantage across generations. When a capable student cannot pursue training because basic fees and living costs are unaffordable, society loses both that individual’s potential and the services they might later provide. Targeted support, for instance, can enable students from low-income households to enter shortage professions without transferring an unnecessary subsidy to families who could fund the same education privately.

Admittedly, universal provision is administratively simple and avoids the stigma sometimes attached to means testing. Yet simplicity is not sufficient if it directs limited resources away from early education, healthcare, or vocational pathways. A transparent threshold and grants that cover essential costs would preserve dignity while keeping the policy financially credible.

Overall, the desirable outcome is not blanket generosity but fair access. By directing support towards people for whom cost is a real obstacle, governments can improve opportunity without weakening other public services.

## Examiner comment
The model gives a direct, nuanced position, develops two connected reasons, and uses an example to clarify a mechanism. Cohesion comes from ideas and reference, not excessive connectors. Vocabulary is precise but not ornamental, and complex sentences remain controlled. A Band 9 response still needs to be accurate, relevant, and natural; length or difficult words alone do not create the score.

## Common mistakes
**Partial answer:** answering only one part of a two-part question. Label each part in your plan.  
**Undeveloped lists:** naming three ideas but explaining none. Use two fully developed points.  
**Memorised language:** forcing an irrelevant phrase or quotation. Write what the question needs.  
**False evidence:** inventing research or statistics. Use plausible examples without pretending authority.  
**New ideas in the conclusion:** conclude; do not begin a third body paragraph.
