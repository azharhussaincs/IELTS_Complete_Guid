## One idea at four levels
Question: Should cities restrict private cars in central areas?

**Band 6:** “Cars cause pollution, so cities should restrict them. People can use buses.” The position is clear but explanation is brief and vocabulary is general.

**Band 7:** “Cities should restrict private cars because concentrated traffic worsens air quality and delays buses. A congestion charge can encourage commuters to use public transport.” The claim is developed and relevant.

**Band 8:** “Restricting private cars in dense centres can improve both air quality and the reliability of buses, which otherwise become trapped in the same congestion. Charging drivers at peak times gives commuters a financial reason to shift journeys.” The mechanism is clear and language is precise.

**Band 9:** “In compact city centres, limiting private vehicles is justified because each additional car consumes scarce road space while slowing buses that carry far more passengers. A peak-time charge, paired with frequent affordable public transport, can therefore reduce emissions without simply transferring inconvenience to lower-income commuters.” The response is nuanced, well developed, and grammatically controlled.

## What changes across bands
Higher bands do not merely add longer sentences. They answer every part, choose ideas that can be explained, connect claims to consequences, use accurate collocations, and make very few errors. Improve one dimension at a time: first relevance, then development, then paragraph cohesion, then language precision.

## Diagnostic routine
Take a recent essay. Highlight thesis statements in blue, explanations in green, examples in yellow, and unsupported assertions in red. If red dominates, develop fewer ideas. Then check whether each pronoun has a clear noun and whether every connector expresses its stated relationship.
