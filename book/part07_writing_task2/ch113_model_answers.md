## How to learn from a model
Do not memorise these responses. Read the question, predict a position and two reasons, then compare your plan with the model. Underline the thesis, topic sentences, explanations, examples, concessions, and conclusion. A model is useful when it teaches decisions, not when it becomes borrowed language.

## Model 1: public transport
**Question:** Governments should make public transport free in all cities. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Free public transport is an attractive proposal because it could reduce the cost of daily travel. However, I do not agree that it should be universally free. A low fare combined with substantial investment in reliability is a more equitable and sustainable policy.

The strongest case for free services is that price can exclude low-income residents from employment, education, and healthcare. When a family must choose between travel and essentials, a fare reduction can have an immediate effect on opportunity. In addition, cheaper buses and trains may persuade some drivers to leave their cars at home, easing congestion and emissions.

Nevertheless, abolishing fares does not itself create a useful network. If services are infrequent, overcrowded, or unavailable outside central areas, many commuters will still need private vehicles. Funding every journey from general taxation may also divert money from maintenance and route expansion. A better approach is to cap fares for regular users and provide free travel for people with demonstrable need, while directing remaining funds towards frequent, accessible services.

In conclusion, affordability matters, but completely free transport is not necessarily the best use of public money. Targeted concessions and high-quality provision would serve both fairness and long-term demand.

**Examiner comment:** The answer maintains a qualified position throughout, develops a counterargument fairly, and evaluates policy by practical criteria. Lexis such as *route expansion* and *targeted concessions* is precise, while grammar is varied and accurate.

## Model 2: remote work
**Question:** More people work from home than in the past. What are the reasons, and is this a positive or negative development?

Working from home has expanded because digital tools now allow many tasks to be completed away from a traditional office, while employers have recognised that physical presence is not always necessary. On balance, this is a positive development, provided workers retain regular contact with colleagues.

The technical reason is straightforward: cloud-based files, video meetings, and secure communication platforms allow teams to coordinate across locations. Employers also save on office space, and staff may avoid long daily commutes. These incentives became visible when organisations were forced to adopt remote systems and later discovered that routine work could continue effectively.

The principal benefit is greater control over time. A parent, for example, may arrange work around school hours without sacrificing employment altogether. Fewer commuting journeys can also reduce pressure on transport networks. Admittedly, isolation and blurred boundaries can harm wellbeing, especially for new employees who need informal guidance. Companies should therefore offer hybrid arrangements, regular mentoring, and a clear right to disconnect.

Overall, remote work is beneficial because it broadens flexibility and can reduce wasted travel. Its advantages depend, however, on deliberate measures to preserve collaboration and protect personal time.

**Examiner comment:** Both parts are answered directly. The response explains mechanisms, offers a realistic example, and makes a balanced evaluation rather than treating the trend as wholly positive.

## Model 3: school assessment
**Question:** Some people think examinations are the best way to assess students, while others believe continuous assessment is better. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Examinations remain popular because they test students under identical conditions, whereas continuous assessment captures performance over a longer period. Although formal exams have a role, I believe a carefully moderated combination of both methods is the fairest approach.

Supporters of examinations argue that a single standardised test limits the influence of teachers’ personal judgement. Students answer the same questions in the same time, making results easier to compare across schools. This can be particularly important for university admission, where institutions need a transparent basis for selecting applicants.

Continuous assessment, however, measures skills that a timed exam may miss. Research projects, presentations, and drafts show whether learners can apply knowledge, respond to feedback, and complete sustained work. They also reduce the risk that illness or anxiety on one day will determine an entire grade. The weakness is that standards can vary, so external moderation is necessary.

In conclusion, examinations offer comparability, while continuous assessment gives a fuller picture of learning. Schools should use both, with common criteria and moderation to protect consistency.

**Examiner comment:** Each view receives a developed paragraph, and the personal view is clear in both introduction and conclusion. The writer avoids unsupported claims and uses a concession to produce a nuanced judgement.

## Model 4: food waste
**Question:** Food waste is increasing in many countries. What problems does this cause, and what can be done?

Rising food waste damages the environment and places unnecessary pressure on household budgets. The problem can be reduced through clearer information, improved retail practices, and systems that redirect edible surplus.

When food is discarded, the water, energy, and labour used to produce and transport it are wasted as well. Disposal creates further emissions, particularly where organic matter is sent to landfill. At household level, frequent disposal often reflects poor planning: consumers buy perishable goods without considering what they already have or when they will use it.

Governments can require clearer date labels that distinguish safety from quality, since many people discard food that remains edible. Retailers should also discount items approaching their use-by date and partner with charities to redistribute safe surplus. Finally, schools and public campaigns can teach practical skills such as meal planning and storage. Together, these measures reduce waste before it reaches the bin rather than relying solely on recycling afterwards.

In conclusion, food waste has environmental and financial costs, but it is not inevitable. Better information and coordinated action by households, shops, and authorities can address it at several stages.

**Examiner comment:** The essay answers problems and solutions in balanced depth. The solutions are specific, feasible, and explicitly linked to the causes.
