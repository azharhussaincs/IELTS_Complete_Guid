## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Apply the **Answer–Extend–Example (AEE)** framework to any Part 1 question.
- Recognise topic clusters and predict follow-up question patterns.
- Avoid over-short and over-long answers while maintaining natural conversation rhythm.
- Use appropriate tenses, pronouns, and vocabulary for familiar personal topics.
- Handle difficult questions (unfamiliar topics, negative preferences) without freezing.
- Build a reusable Part 1 preparation bank without memorising fixed scripts.
- Self-correct and paraphrase smoothly when you make mid-answer errors.

## Theory / Explanation

### 115.1 The Purpose of Part 1

Part 1 lasts **4–5 minutes** and functions as a **warm-up**. The examiner asks about **familiar topics** related to your life: home, hometown, work, study, hobbies, daily routines, food, transport, weather, and similar themes.

**What Part 1 is not:**
- A formality you can ignore
- A vocabulary showcase competition
- An opportunity to recite a memorised life story

**What Part 1 is:**
- Evidence of whether you can maintain **basic fluency and coherence** under light pressure
- A sample of your **pronunciation** and **grammar in simple contexts**
- The psychological foundation for Parts 2 and 3

Candidates who treat Part 1 as "easy" and answer minimally often **enter Part 2 nervous and already scored down** on FC.

### 115.2 Question Types in Part 1

Part 1 questions fall into predictable categories:

| Type | Example | Response need |
|------|---------|---------------|
| Factual | "Where do you live?" | Clear direct fact |
| Preference | "Do you prefer tea or coffee?" | Choice + reason |
| Frequency | "How often do you exercise?" | Time expression + detail |
| Experience | "Have you ever…?" | Yes/no + narrative snippet |
| Opinion (mild) | "Do you think… is important?" | View + brief justification |
| Hypothetical (rare) | "If you could change one thing about your city…" | Conditional answer |

**Follow-up pattern:** Examiners typically ask **2–4 questions on one topic** before switching. Your first answer often determines follow-up depth.

### 115.3 The AEE Framework

The most reliable Part 1 structure is **Answer–Extend–Example**:

1. **Answer** — Respond directly in the first sentence (yes/no/what/where).
2. **Extend** — Add reason, detail, comparison, or contrast.
3. **Example** — One concrete personal instance (past or recent).

**Template (flexible, not memorised):**
> *[Direct answer]. [Reason or detail]. [For example / Actually / Last week], [specific instance].*

**Why AEE works:** It satisfies FC (development), LR (range), and GRA (compound sentences) without unnatural length. Target **20–40 seconds** per answer — roughly **2–4 sentences**.

### 115.4 Optimal Answer Length

| Length | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| 1 word | Band 4–5 signal; avoid |
| 1 sentence | Minimal; Band 5–6 unless very rich |
| 2–4 sentences | Target zone for Band 6–8 |
| 5+ sentences | Often unnatural; examiner may interrupt |

**Rule of thumb:** Stop when you have answered, extended, and exemplified. Do not wait for silence.

### 115.5 Topic Clusters and Preparation

IELTS rotates topics but themes recur. Prepare **ideas**, not scripts:

**Cluster A — Home & locality**
- Type of home, neighbourhood, favourite room, changes you'd make
- Hometown: location, size, what you like/dislike, visitors

**Cluster B — Work & study**
- Job/major, why chosen, daily tasks, future plans
- Study habits, favourite subject, school/university facilities

**Cluster C — Leisure & lifestyle**
- Hobbies, sports, reading, music, cooking, shopping
- Weekends, holidays, time with friends/family

**Cluster D — Daily life**
- Transport, sleep, meals, technology use, weather
- Morning routine, organisation, punctuality

**Preparation method:** For each cluster, prepare **3 stories** (30 seconds each) you can adapt. Example: a weekend trip story works for holidays, hobbies, and time with friends.

### 115.6 Tense and Grammar Choices

Part 1 tests basic tense control:

| Question time frame | Typical tense |
|---------------------|---------------|
| Current state | Present simple/continuous |
| Habits | Present simple + frequency adverbs |
| Past experience | Past simple; past continuous for background |
| Future plans | *going to* / *will* / present continuous for arrangements |
| Preferences | Present + *would prefer* |

**Common errors:**
- *"I am living here since 2019"* → *"I've lived here since 2019"*
- *"I am agree"* → *"I agree"*
- Switching mid-answer from past to present without signal

**Safe complex structures for Part 1:**
- *"Although I enjoy X, I don't do it as often as I'd like because…"*
- *"The main reason I prefer X is that…"*
- *"If I had more free time, I'd probably…"* (hypothetical — use accurately)

### 115.7 Lexical Resource in Part 1

You do not need obscure vocabulary. You need **precise, natural** words:

| Weak | Stronger |
|------|----------|
| good | enjoyable / convenient / refreshing |
| bad | overcrowded / unreliable / exhausting |
| like | fond of / keen on / attracted to |
| very big | spacious / vast |
| things | items / activities / aspects |

**Collocations matter:** *heavy traffic, grab a coffee, catch the bus, stuck indoors, get some fresh air.*

**Avoid:** Forcing idioms (*"hit the nail on the head"*) into every answer; misusing *"Nowadays"* as a crutch opener.

### 115.8 Handling Negative Answers

Questions about dislikes or things you don't do are **normal**:

**Question:** "Do you enjoy cooking?"  
**Honest negative:** "Not especially — I'm quite lazy in the kitchen, to be honest. I usually order delivery or eat at my parents' place on Sundays. That said, I can make a decent omelette when I have to."

**Why this works:** Direct honesty + extension + slight positive = natural, fluent answer. Never invent passion you cannot sustain in follow-ups.

### 115.9 Handling Unfamiliar Topics

If asked about something you rarely consider (e.g., singing, museums):

1. **Acknowledge honestly:** *"I don't do that very often, actually."*
2. **Redirect with related experience:** *"But I do enjoy listening to music when I commute."*
3. **Speculate lightly:** *"I suppose I'd like to try it if I had a patient teacher."*

**Do not:** Say only "I don't know" and stop.

### 115.10 Memorisation Traps

Examiners detect:
- **Scripted intros** unrelated to the question
- **Topic mismatch** (answer about "travel" when asked about "reading")
- **Flat intonation** on supposedly personal content
- **Grammar too perfect** for spontaneous speech followed by breakdown in Part 3

**Safe approach:** Memorise **structures and phrases**, not full answers. Rotate examples.

### 115.11 Interaction Skills

Part 1 is a dialogue:
- **Listen** to the full question; answer what was asked.
- **Don't interrupt** the examiner.
- **Eye contact** (cultural norms permitting) shows confidence.
- **React naturally** to surprising questions — brief *"That's an interesting question"* is acceptable once per test, not every turn.

### 115.12 Common Part 1 Errors by Band

| Band area | Typical Part 1 behaviour |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| 5 | Mostly short; repetition; limited vocabulary |
| 6 | Willing to extend but formulaic; some errors |
| 7 | Fluent with occasional hesitation; varied vocabulary |
| 8+ | Natural, flexible, precise; smooth self-correction |

## Examples

### Example 1: AEE Applied — Work

**Q:** "Do you work or are you a student?"  
**A:** "I'm working full-time as a junior accountant at a logistics firm."  
**E:** "I chose accounting because I was always good with numbers, and this industry is quite fast-paced, which suits me."  
**Ex:** "For instance, last month I helped reconcile shipping invoices for three warehouses — it was stressful but satisfying."

### Example 2: Preference Question

**Q:** "Do you prefer hot or cold weather?"  
**Answer:** "Definitely cold weather — I find heat draining, especially when humidity is high. In cold months I can layer up and stay active outdoors. Summer where I live gets uncomfortable; I tend to stay indoors with the air conditioning on."

### Example 3: Negative Preference

**Q:** "Do you like shopping?"  
**Answer:** "Not really — crowded malls overwhelm me, and I hate queuing. I shop online for clothes and only visit physical stores when I need to try something on, like shoes."

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Turning a Band 5 Answer into Band 7

**Question:** "How often do you use your phone?"

**Band 5:** "Every day. Many times."

**Revision steps:**
1. Add frequency phrase: *"Pretty much constantly throughout the day."*
2. Add reason: *"I need it for work messages and navigation."*
3. Add example/limit: *"I try to put it away after nine p.m., though, so I can sleep properly."*

**Band 7 target:** "I'd say I'm on my phone throughout the day — mainly for work emails and maps when I'm travelling between sites. I do try to disconnect after nine in the evening because otherwise I struggle to fall asleep."

### Worked Example 2: Follow-Up Chain

**Topic:** Hometown

| # | Question | Strategy |
|---|----------|----------|
| 1 | Where's your hometown? | Direct location + size |
| 2 | What do you like about it? | One like + example place |
| 3 | Has it changed much? | Compare past/present |
| 4 | Would you live there in future? | Opinion + condition |

**Planning note:** Question 3 and 4 can share the same example (new metro system) if developed differently.

### Worked Example 3: Self-Correction Without Panic

**Mid-answer error:** *"I have went to the gym…"*

**Self-correction:** *"Sorry — I **went** to the gym yesterday, actually, for a yoga class."*

**WHY:** Light self-correction shows GRA awareness; apologising repeatedly hurts FC.

## Review

- Part 1 = **4–5 minutes**, familiar topics, **2–4 sentences** per answer.
- Use **AEE**: Answer, Extend, Example.
- Prepare **topic clusters**, not scripts.
- Negative/unfamiliar answers are fine if developed honestly.
- Tense accuracy and natural vocabulary beat rare words.
- Listen and answer the **actual question**.

## Exam Tips

> **Exam Strategy:** Your **first sentence** must answer the question — examiners note directness immediately.

> **Exam Strategy:** If you use a **prepared example**, adapt it to the exact wording — mismatch is a memorisation red flag.

> **Exam Strategy:** **Don't ask** "Should I say more?" — extend naturally; the examiner will interrupt if needed.

> **Exam Strategy:** Warm up your voice **before** the test — hum, read aloud, or chat in English for 10 minutes.

## Band 9 Tips

> **Band 9 Secret:** Band 9 Part 1 answers feel **effortlessly personal** — small specific details (*"a café on the corner that closes at four"*) without rehearsed polish.

> **Band 9 Secret:** High-band speakers **vary sentence openings** — not every answer begins with "Yes, I…" or "Well, I think…"

> **Band 9 Secret:** They **match register to topic** — casual tone for hobbies, slightly more formal for work/study, never stiff or academic in Part 1.

### Band 9 Model — Part 1 Mini-Set (original)

**Q:** "Do you prefer texting or calling?"  
**A:** "It depends on the situation, really. If I need a quick confirmation — like running late for dinner — I'll text. But if something's complicated or emotional, I'd rather call because tone gets lost in messages. My mother, for instance, still prefers long phone calls on Sundays, and I've got used to that ritual."

**Q:** "Has your preference changed over time?"  
**A:** "Definitely. When I was a teenager I avoided phone calls entirely; now I appreciate them more, especially for staying in touch with relatives abroad. Video calls have become part of that too — we schedule them so time zones align."

**WHY Band 9:** Natural qualification, clear examples, flexible grammar, zero strain.
