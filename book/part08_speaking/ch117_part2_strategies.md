## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Use the **one-minute preparation time** efficiently to build a speakable note map.
- Structure a **1–2 minute monologue** that addresses all cue card bullet points without sounding read aloud.
- Apply flexible narrative frameworks (PPF, STAR, timeline) to diverse cue card topics.
- Manage timing so you speak for at least **90 seconds** and up to 2 minutes confidently.
- Handle abstract or unfamiliar cue cards by adapting prepared story skeletons.
- Maintain fluency when you lose your place or forget a planned detail.
- Respond appropriately to Part 2 rounding-off questions.

## Theory / Explanation

### 117.1 The Purpose of Part 2

Part 2 is the **individual long turn**. You receive a cue card, prepare for **1 minute**, then speak for **1–2 minutes** without interruption from the examiner (except to signal time).

**What Part 2 tests:**
- Ability to **speak at length** on your own
- **Organisation** and coherence of extended speech
- Range of **vocabulary and grammar** in monologue mode
- **Pronunciation** sustained over time

Part 2 often produces the **clearest band signal** because the examiner hears uninterrupted production. Weak Part 2 performance is difficult to recover from in Part 3.

### 117.2 Cue Card Anatomy

Typical format:

```
Describe [person/place/event/object/experience/skill/etc.]
You should say:
- [bullet 1 — usually what/who]
- [bullet 2 — when/where/how]
- [bullet 3 — detail or relationship]
and explain [bullet 4 — why/feeling/significance]
```

**Optional line:** *"You will have one minute to prepare… You have one to two minutes to speak. Don't worry if I stop you…"*

**Critical rule:** Address **all bullets**. Missing one bullet caps Task achievement equivalent in FC — the examiner notices gaps.

### 117.3 The One-Minute Preparation Protocol

**Seconds 0–10:** Read entire card twice. Underline **topic** and **explain** line.

**Seconds 10–40:** Write **note map** (not sentences):
- Keywords only
- One word triggers memory
- Arrows for sequence

**Seconds 40–55:** Mentally rehearse **opening sentence** and **closing sentence**.

**Seconds 55–60:** Look up; breathe; begin when examiner indicates.

**What not to do during prep:**
- Write full sentences (wastes time)
- Memorise an essay silently (causes robotic delivery)
- Panic if topic is unfamiliar (adapt a story skeleton)

### 117.4 Note-Mapping Templates

**Timeline map** (events, trips, life changes):
```
Past → Event → Detail → Present impact
2019 | Kyoto | temples | still recommend
```

**PPF map** (Past–Present–Future) — works for habits, places, people:
```
Past: child — grandma's kitchen
Present: cook weekly — pasta
Future: teach niece
```

**STAR map** (Situation–Task–Action–Result) — skills, achievements:
```
S: office slow reports
T: learn Excel macros
A: online course 3 months
R: saved 5 hrs/week
```

**Sensory map** (places, objects):
```
See: golden roof
Hear: busy market
Feel: humid air
Meaning: first solo trip
```

### 117.5 Monologue Structure Frameworks

**Framework A — Classic narrative (4 blocks)**

1. **Introduction (15–20 sec):** State topic; set scene.
2. **Body 1 (25–30 sec):** First bullet + vivid detail.
3. **Body 2 (25–30 sec):** Second/third bullets woven together.
4. **Conclusion (15–20 sec):** "Explain" bullet — feelings, significance, recommendation.

**Framework B — Topic sentence per bullet**

Each bullet becomes one **mini-paragraph** of 2–3 sentences. Simple and reliable for Band 6–7.

**Framework C — Thematic depth**

Choose **one theme** (e.g., "learning independence") and thread all bullets through it. Stronger for Band 8+ if controlled.

### 117.6 Opening and Closing Sentences

**Strong openings (adapt, don't memorise one):**
- *"I'd like to talk about…"*
- *"The [person/place] I'd like to describe is…"*
- *"Something that comes to mind is…"*

**Strong closings:**
- *"So overall, that's why this experience stayed with me."*
- *"Looking back, I'd definitely recommend it because…"*
- *"That's the main reason it mattered so much to me."*

**Avoid:** *"That's all I can say"* (signals inability to extend).

### 117.7 Timing Management

| Duration | Assessment signal |
|----------|-------------------|
| Under 1 min | Insufficient FC for Band 7+ |
| 1:00–1:30 | Adequate minimum |
| 1:30–2:00 | Strong target |
| Forced stop at 2:00 | Good — examiner had more to listen to |

**Practice method:** Record 10 cue cards; log duration. If under 1:30, add **sensory detail** or **comparison** (*"Unlike other cities I've visited…"*).

### 117.8 Vocabulary and Grammar for Part 2

Part 2 invites **descriptive language**:

| Function | Language |
|----------|----------|
| Setting scene | *"It was a chilly autumn morning when…"*
| Emphasis | *"What struck me most was…"*
| Feelings | *"I felt overwhelmed / relieved / proud"*
| Comparison | *"Compared with my expectations…"*
| Reason | *"The main reason I chose it was…"*

**Grammar range:**
- Past narrative: past simple + past continuous + past perfect (one instance)
- Relative clauses: *"The friend who recommended it…"*
- Participle clauses: *"Having never tried it before, I was nervous."*

**Balance:** Two complex structures well-used beat five broken ones.

### 117.9 When the Topic Is Unfamiliar

**Strategy 1 — Closest true story:** Cue card asks about a museum you haven't visited → describe a **gallery or historical site** you have.

**Strategy 2 — Hypothetical frame (careful):** *"I haven't done this often, but one experience that fits is…"*

**Strategy 3 — Person/object pivot:** *"Describe a law"* → talk about a **rule at school/work** that affected you (if genuinely relevant).

**Never:** Invent an implausible celebrity-dinner story; follow-ups in Part 3 may expose inconsistency.

### 117.10 Delivery: Notes vs Eye Contact

- Glance at notes **briefly**; speak to examiner.
- Notes are **triggers**, not a script to read.
- If you lose place, pause, look at notes, resume: *"Where was I — ah yes, the second point…"*

### 117.11 Rounding-Off Questions

After Part 2, examiner asks **1–2 short questions** requiring brief answers:

- *"Would you like to do that again?"*
- *"Do your friends enjoy that too?"*

**Answer in 1–2 sentences.** Do not restart a new monologue.

### 117.12 Common Part 2 Failure Patterns

| Error | Fix |
|-------|-----|
| Stops at 45 seconds | Pre-plan closing; add example |
| Reads notes word-for-word | Keyword notes only |
| Ignores a bullet | Check off bullets while practising |
| Lists without linking | Use sequencers: *"After that… Later…"*
| Flat tone | Vary stress; pause before key points |
| Wrong tense throughout | Mark tense on notes (P/S/F) |

### 117.13 Cue Card Topic Categories

Although every cue card is unique, most fit **recurring categories**. Recognising the category during your 1-minute prep helps you choose the right framework instantly.

| Category | Typical verbs | Best framework | Example topics |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|
| Person | describe someone, a teacher, a friend | PPF or character arc | influential person, family member, famous person |
| Place | describe a city, building, quiet place | Sensory + timeline | hometown, café, museum, natural site |
| Event | describe a celebration, accident, achievement | Timeline narrative | wedding, festival, sports event |
| Object | describe a gift, book, photo, device | Description + significance | phone, photograph, traditional product |
| Activity | describe a hobby, sport, skill | STAR or PPF | cooking skill, language learned, project |
| Experience | describe a trip, meal, challenge | Timeline + reflection | journey, difficult decision, proud moment |
| Abstract | describe a law, tradition, goal | Thematic + example | important rule, future ambition, tradition |

**Cross-category adaptation:** A "person" story can answer an "event" card if the event centred on that person. A "place" story can answer "experience" if you frame it as *"an experience that took place in…"*

### 117.14 The 90-Second Safety Net

If you tend to finish early, pre-load a **safety net layer** you add only when time remains:

1. **Comparison:** *"Compared with other places I've visited…"*
2. **Sensory detail:** one sound, smell, or texture
3. **Emotional reflection:** *"Looking back, I realise…"*
4. **Recommendation:** *"I'd suggest going in… / avoiding…"*
5. **Future link:** *"I'd like to return / I'd never repeat…"*

Practise delivering the core story in 75 seconds, then attach one safety-net layer to reach 95 seconds. In the exam, if you hit 1:45 naturally, skip the safety net — do not pad mechanically.

### 117.15 Part 2 and Part 3 Handoff

Part 2 content **feeds** Part 3 but should not **repeat** it. During prep, note one **general theme** from your story (e.g., solo travel → independence; coding skill → lifelong learning). When Part 3 opens, shift register from personal narrative to societal analysis. Mentally "close" Part 2 with a clean ending so Part 3 feels like a new conversation, not an extension of the monologue.

## Examples

### Example 1: Note Map

**Cue card:** Describe a place you visited on holiday.

```
Place: coastal town — Essaouira, Morocco
When: 2022, spring, 4 days
Who: solo + met photographer
Why impressed: medina, wind, seafood, calm pace
Recommend: yes — artists, not party town
```

### Example 2: Opening + Body Extract

*"I'd like to talk about Essaouira, a coastal town in Morocco I visited in spring 2022. I went alone for four days — my first proper solo trip — and I'd seen photos of the old medina walls against the Atlantic, so I was curious. What impressed me most was the contrast between the busy blue-and-white alleys inside the walls and the wide, windy beach outside, where kite surfers gather every afternoon."*

### Example 3: Closing Extract

*"So overall, it left me with this sense of calm I don't usually get on holiday. I'd recommend it to anyone who wants culture and sea air without the intensity of bigger tourist cities — though I'd suggest going in spring before the summer crowds arrive."*

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Bullet Coverage Check

**Cue card bullets vs speech plan:**

| Bullet | Covered? | Speech segment |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| what place | ✓ | Opening sentence |
| when you went | ✓ | Body 1 |
| who with | ✓ | Body 1 |
| why impressed | ✓ | Body 2 |
| explain recommend | ✓ | Closing |

**Self-check habit:** Tick bullets on cue card during practice.

### Worked Example 2: Extending from 1:05 to 1:45

**Original stop point:** Finished story after main event.

**Additions:**
- Sensory detail (+15 sec): sounds, smells, weather
- Comparison (+10 sec): *"Unlike package tours I've done…"*
- Reflection (+15 sec): what you learned / how you changed

### Worked Example 3: Recovery After Freeze

**Situation:** Forgot friend's name at 1:00 mark.

**Recovery:** *"My friend — I'm blanking on her surname, but we were at university together — suggested we try this restaurant. Anyway, the food was…"*

**WHY:** Brief honest recovery beats long silent panic.

## Review

- Part 2 = **1 min prep + 1–2 min speak**.
- Notes = **keywords**, not scripts.
- Cover **every bullet** explicitly.
- Target **90+ seconds**; aim for natural stop at 2:00.
- Use frameworks: narrative, PPF, STAR, sensory.
- Rounding-off = **short** answers.
- Practise with timer and recording.

## Exam Tips

> **Exam Strategy:** The **"explain" line** on the cue card is your closing — plan it during prep even if you speak it last.

> **Exam Strategy:** If the examiner **stops you**, that's often positive — you filled the time.

> **Exam Strategy:** Put **tense markers** on notes: P / Pr / F — prevents mid-story tense chaos.

> **Exam Strategy:** Breathe at bullet **transitions** — micro-pauses sound natural, not hesitant.

## Band 9 Tips

> **Band 9 Secret:** Band 9 Part 2 speeches **sound discovered, not delivered** — the candidate appears to relive the experience with selective vivid detail.

> **Band 9 Secret:** They use **one well-chosen anecdote** rather than listing ten facts.

> **Band 9 Secret:** Discourse markers are **invisible** — no mechanical *"Firstly… Secondly…"* unless topic truly requires sequence.

### Band 9 Model — Part 2 Extract (original cue card: Describe a quiet place)

*"I'd like to describe a small public library branch about ten minutes from my apartment — not the main city library, but a converted house on a residential street. I stumbled on it during the pandemic when cafés were closed and I couldn't focus at home. What makes it quiet isn't silence — you sometimes hear pages or a kettle in the staff room — but the sense that nobody expects anything of you. The reading room has these low wooden desks by a window overlooking a garden, and in winter the radiator ticks, which oddly helps me concentrate. I still go there when I need to think through a difficult email or read without my phone. I'd recommend it to anyone who finds open-plan offices or noisy co-working spaces draining — though I'd mention it closes early on Sundays, so timing matters."*

**Duration:** ~1:50 | **Bullets:** all covered | **Features:** sensory detail, reflection, qualified recommendation.
