## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Define **fluency** and **coherence** as IELTS assesses them — distinct from "speed" or "talking a lot."
- Reduce harmful hesitation, repetition, and self-repair while keeping natural thinking pauses.
- Use **discourse markers** and cohesive devices appropriately across Parts 1, 2, and 3.
- Structure extended answers so listeners can follow without confusion.
- Apply practical drills to increase **speakable length** under time pressure.
- Distinguish fluency problems from grammar or vocabulary problems in self-diagnosis.
- Maintain coherence when you change direction or forget planned content.

## Theory / Explanation

### 122.1 Fluency vs Coherence — Definitions

**Fluency** (in IELTS FC): Ability to speak at length **without undue effort**, undue repetition, or long pauses. Includes:
- Pace (not necessarily fast)
- Smooth delivery
- Willingness to extend

**Coherence**: Logical **organisation** and **clear progression** of ideas. Listeners follow **why** one sentence follows another. Includes:
- Sequencing
- Referencing (*this, that, the former*)
- Paragraph-like structure in monologues

**Critical distinction:** A fast speaker can be **incoherent**; a slow speaker can be **highly coherent**. Band descriptors reward **both**.

### 122.2 What Counts as "Hesitation"

Not all pauses are equal:

| Type | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Brief content pause (1 sec) | Natural; planning next idea |
| Language search pause + filler loop | Hurts fluency |
| Mid-clause freeze | Hurts FC and sometimes P |
| Repetition (*"I think I think"*) | Band 6 or below signal |
| False start + clean restart | Acceptable if rare |

**Band 7 descriptor language:** "speaks at length without noticeable effort **or loss of coherence**" — occasional hesitation OK.

**Band 5:** "cannot respond without noticeable pauses"; "often self-corrects."

### 122.3 The Hesitation Hierarchy — Fixes

**Level 1 — Fillers (use sparingly)**
- Acceptable once: *"Well," "Let me think," "That's interesting."*
- Overuse: *"Um, uh, like, you know"* every clause — reduce through recording awareness.

**Level 2 — Paraphrase instead of freeze**
- Can't find *" ubiquitous"* → *"you see it everywhere"*
- Fluency > rare word.

**Level 3 — Structural escape**
- Lost in Part 2 → glance at notes, resume: *"Another point about…"*
- Part 3 → answer at simpler level first, then add layer.

**Level 4 — Practice automaticity**
- High-frequency personal topics (Part 1) should need **minimal** planning pauses.

### 122.4 Coherence Devices — Overview

| Device | Function | Example |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Sequencers | Time/order | *First, Then, After that, Finally* |
| Additive | Add idea | *Moreover, In addition, Also* |
| Contrast | Oppose | *However, On the other hand, Whereas* |
| Cause/effect | Explain | *Therefore, As a result, Because of that* |
| Example | Illustrate | *For instance, Such as, To give you an example* |
| Summary | Close | *Overall, In short, So basically* |
| Reference | Link back | *This experience, That approach, The former* |

**Part-appropriate use:**

| Part | Coherence priority |
|------|-------------------|
| Part 1 | Light sequencers; don't over-signpost |
| Part 2 | Clear arc; time sequencers; sensory layering |
| Part 3 | Contrast + cause + qualification |

### 122.5 Discourse Marker Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Problem |
|---------|---------|
| Mechanical listing | *"Firstly… Secondly… Thirdly…"* in Part 1 feels robotic |
| Wrong marker | *"However"* without contrast |
| Over-signposting | More markers than content |
| Memorised chunks | *"In this day and age"* every answer |

**Band 8+:** Markers are **sparse and accurate** — listener notices logic, not templates.

### 122.6 Extending Answers — SPEEL Framework

When answers are too short, extend with **SPEEL**:

| Letter | Move | Example prompt |
|--------|------|----------------|
| S | Specify | Names, numbers, places |
| P | Personal reaction | *"I felt… / I was surprised…"* |
| E | Example | Past incident |
| E | Explain why | Reason/cause |
| L | Link forward | *"Which is why I…"* |

Works in Part 1 and Part 2; Part 3 uses **Explain + Example** most.

### 122.7 Part-Specific Fluency Strategies

#### Part 1
- Target **automatic** 3-sentence answers on 20 common topics.
- Reduce **question echo** (*"Do I like cooking? Yes…"*) — wastes time.

#### Part 2
- **One-minute rule:** If you finish a bullet in 15 seconds, add detail before moving on.
- Use **past narrative flow**: background → event → reflection.
- **Closing flag:** *"So that's why…"* signals completion to examiner.

#### Part 3
- **Direct answer first** — coherence starts with relevance.
- **One idea per sentence** — complex ideas split across sentences.
- **Return to question** if you drift: *"So to answer your question directly…"*

### 122.8 Repetition and Self-Correction

**Harmful repetition:**
- Same word 5× (*"good good good"*)
- Same structure every answer (*"I think… I think…"*)

**Fix:** Synonym circle practice; alternate openers.

**Self-correction:**
- **Good:** *"I went there two years — sorry, three years ago."*
- **Bad:** *"Sorry sorry sorry, my English is bad, um, I mean…"* (confidence collapse)

**Rule:** One correction, continue.

### 122.9 Fluency Building Drills

#### Drill 1 — 4-3-2
Same topic: speak 4 min → 3 min → 2 min with partner. Forces compression and prioritisation.

#### Drill 2 — Timer extension
Answer with 1 sentence → partner says *"more"* until 30 seconds reached.

#### Drill 3 — No-pause read-aloud
Read graded text smoothly; transfer to own stories.

#### Drill 4 — Random topic cards
Pick topic; speak 90 sec with only 5 sec prep — builds FC under pressure.

#### Drill 5 — Filler audit
Record 3 minutes; count fillers; reduce by 50% next attempt.

### 122.10 Coherence Failure Patterns

| Pattern | Listener experience |
|---------|---------------------|
| Topic drift | "What was the question?" |
| Missing closure | Part 2 feels unfinished |
| Non-sequitur example | Example doesn't prove claim |
| Tense shift chaos | Timeline confused |
| Stack of unrelated ideas | Part 3 essay without glue |

**Fix:** **Outline mentally** before Part 3 answers; **bullet map** in Part 2 prep.

### 122.11 Fluency vs Grammar Trade-Off

Under pressure, candidates choose:
- **Safe simple sentences** → fluent but Band 6 GRA ceiling
- **Risky complex sentences** → errors + pauses → Band 6 FC ceiling

**Optimal:** Pre-practise **safe complex templates**:
- *"Although X, Y."*
- *"One reason is… Another is…"*
- *"If I hadn't…, I wouldn't…"*

Automatic templates free cognitive load for content.

### 122.12 Measuring Progress

Track weekly mocks:

| Metric | Band 6 target | Band 7 target |
|--------|---------------|---------------|
| Part 1 avg answer length | 20+ sec | 25–40 sec |
| Part 2 duration | 1:15+ | 1:45–2:00 |
| Fillers per minute | <8 | <4 |
| Self-corrections per part | <5 | 2–3 |
| Coherence breaks (partner flags) | <3 | 0–1 |

### 122.13 Reference and Ellipsis for Coherence

Advanced coherence uses **reference words** to link back without repetition:

| Device | Example |
|--------|---------|
| this/that | *"I tried online courses. **That** approach suited my schedule."* |
| such | *"It was a **such** a steep learning curve that I nearly quit."* |
| the former/latter | *"Tea calms me; coffee energises me. I prefer **the former** at night."* |
| one/ones | *"I've visited many coastal towns, but Essaouira is **the one** I remember most."* |
| ellipsis | *"Some prefer cities; others, the countryside."* (ellipsis of *prefer*) |

**Part 2 application:** After introducing a place, refer to *"there"* or *"the town"* instead of repeating the name six times.

**Part 3 application:** *"That policy"* / *"This trend"* assumes the listener tracks your argument — but only if your previous sentence established the referent clearly.

### 122.14 When Fluency Breaks Down — Recovery Scripts

Memorise **three recovery scripts** (structure only, not fixed wording):

1. **Word search:** *"I'm trying to think of the word — I mean, the situation where…"* then paraphrase.
2. **Lost thread (Part 2):** *"Let me go back to the second point on the card…"* glance at notes.
3. **Part 3 overload:** *"There are probably several reasons — the main one is…"* then give **one** reason well.

Recovery executed calmly **protects** FC; apologising repeatedly **damages** it.

### 122.15 FC Integration Across the Full Test

Think of FC as a **single arc** across 11–14 minutes:

| Phase | FC priority |
|-------|-------------|
| Part 1 opening | Warm, steady pace — no rush |
| Part 1 → Part 2 transition | Brief pause OK; examiner introduces card |
| Part 2 | Sustained monologue coherence |
| Part 2 → Part 3 | Shift from narrative to analysis — register change |
| Part 3 late questions | Maintain energy; avoid shortening answers from fatigue |

**Fatigue management:** Part 3 answers often shrink after minute 12. Practise **full-length mocks** so Part 3 quality holds when tired. Hydrate before the test; shallow breathing tightens speech.

## Examples

### Example 1: Coherent Part 1 Progression

**Q:** "Do you like reading?"  
**A:** "Yes — I'm quite an avid reader, mostly non-fiction." *(answer)*  
**E:** "I find it relaxing before bed, and it helps me learn about topics I wouldn't study formally." *(extend)*  
**Ex:** "Last month I read a book about urban planning, which oddly connected to a podcast I'd heard." *(example + link)*

### Example 2: Part 2 Sequencing

*"**First**, I'll mention where this place is — it's a lake about two hours north of here. **Then**, in terms of when I went, it was last autumn, when the foliage was at its peak. **What impressed me most** was how quiet it was compared with coastal resorts. **So overall**, it's somewhere I'd return to for a digital detox."*

### Example 3: Part 3 Qualification

*"I **do think** remote work offers flexibility; **however**, it can blur boundaries between home and office. **As a result**, some people end up working longer hours. **For example**, colleagues of mine check email late at night. **That said**, with clear policies, the benefits usually outweigh the drawbacks."*

## Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Reducing Fillers

**Before (30 sec):** 12 *"um"* instances.

**Process:**
1. Transcribe recording
2. Circle fillers
3. Re-record with **pause instead of um** — silent 0.5 sec OK

**After:** 4 fillers; same content; Band +0.5 FC typical in practice.

### Worked Example 2: Coherence Repair Mid-Part-2

**Drift:** Started describing a restaurant; talks about unrelated work stress.

**Repair:** *"Sorry — I'm drifting — the main point about the restaurant was its atmosphere, which helped me unwind after stressful weeks at work."*

**WHY:** Explicit repair shows coherence awareness; better than continuing drift.

### Worked Example 3: Extending Band 5 Part 1

**Q:** "Do you use social media?"

**Band 5:** "Yes, I use Instagram every day."

**SPEEL pass:**
- **S:** Instagram, mainly stories  
- **P:** I enjoy seeing friends' updates  
- **E:** Yesterday a cousin posted wedding photos  
- **E:** It keeps me connected abroad  
- **L:** So I probably spend too much time scrolling

**Result:** 4 sentences, coherent, fluent.

## Solutions (with WHY)

### Set B (samples)

9. *"I like cities; **however**, they can be noisy at night."*  
10. *"Many people learn languages — **for instance**, my sister learned Spanish online."*  
11. *"**Because** public transport is cheap, I use the metro most days."*  
12. *"**First** I packed, **then** I flew, and **after that** I checked into the hotel."*  
13. *"**Overall**, the trip was tiring but worth it."*  
14. *"I prefer tea; **the latter** keeps me awake less than coffee."* (or: *"Tea, not coffee — **that** keeps me awake."*)

### Set C

## Review

- Fluency ≠ speed; coherence ≠ using many linkers.
- **Brief pauses OK**; filler loops and mid-clause freezes hurt.
- **SPEEL** extends short answers; **OEE** structures Part 3 (Chapter 119).
- Discourse markers must be **accurate and sparse**.
- Self-correct **once**, move on.
- Record metrics: duration, fillers, coherence breaks.

## Exam Tips

> **Exam Strategy:** **Silence** beats *"um um um"* — examiners prefer brief thinking pause to filler chains.

> **Exam Strategy:** In Part 2, if you run out at 1:10, have a **backup layer**: feelings + recommendation + comparison.

> **Exam Strategy:** **Topic drift** hurts more than simple grammar errors — return to question explicitly.

> **Exam Strategy:** Practise **same topics** until Part 1 answers feel automatic — saves FC for Part 3.

## Band 9 Tips

> **Band 9 Secret:** Band 9 fluency looks **effortless** because content is organised before speech starts — even Part 3 answers have invisible micro-outlines.

> **Band 9 Secret:** They **never panic-fill** — if an idea is missing, they simplify the claim and continue.

> **Band 9 Secret:** Coherence comes from **logic**, not linker density — one *"however"* at the right moment beats five misplaced transitions.

### Band 9 Model — FC Annotated (original Part 3 fragment)

*"**To answer directly**, I don't think traditional libraries will disappear — **rather**, their role is shifting. **Instead of** being the only source of books, they function as community hubs **where** people study, access wifi, and attend workshops. **Take** my local branch **as an example**: half the floor space is now a children's reading zone. **That said**, funding is a real issue; **without** municipal support, smaller branches **might** close. **On balance**, though, the social function **keeps** them relevant."*

**FC features:** direct opening, contrast, example, qualification, balanced close — markers serve logic.
